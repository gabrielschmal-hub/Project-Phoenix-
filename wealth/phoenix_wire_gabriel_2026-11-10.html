In five minutesthree things
1October CPI came in hotter than expected. Prices rose 0.4% on the month against 0.3% expected, and the core 0.3% against 0.2%, a third straight 0.3% core month. Shelter made up more than 30% of the headline rise. There is no official annual rate this month because of last year's data gap. The headline did cool from September's 0.6%.
2The bond market moved more than stocks. The two-year yield rose about 17bp to 4.73% and the ten-year about 10bp to 4.98%, after briefly topping 5%, on a soft 10-year auction. A December hike is now close to fully priced: about 88% for a quarter-point and 11% for a half-point on CME's FedWatch. Governor Waller said a half-point should be considered if inflation runs hot, and Dallas's Logan said two more hikes will be needed.
3Stocks dipped and Nvidia fell again. The S&P fell 0.38%, the Nasdaq 0.74% and the Dow 0.30%, with technology down 1.15% and breadth almost even. Nvidia fell 5.47% to 168.89, a second day of losses during its board investigation, and Microsoft 2.20%. Gold fell 2.61%, and Brent briefly dipped below $90 for the first time since August.

Sigma (σ) measures a move against that instrument’s own recent behaviour, not against zero. One sigma is a normal day. Two sigma is a move bigger than roughly 95% of its last twenty sessions — rare enough to be information rather than noise.

What actually matteredone session, filtered

A hot CPI makes December a question of size, not timing

1. Inflation is not slowing enough. A 0.4% headline and a third straight 0.3% core month, with shelter doing much of the work, is the kind of report that keeps a central bank hiking. It came after 165,000 jobs on Friday.

2. The debate is now 25 or 50. Waller, usually one of the more cautious governors, said a half-point should be considered if inflation runs hot; Logan wants two more hikes. Futures price about 88% for a quarter-point and 11% for a half-point in December. The two-year rose about 17bp, its biggest move in weeks.

3. Stocks absorbed it, Nvidia did not. The S&P fell only 0.38%, and breadth was even. The damage was concentrated in technology, with Nvidia down 5.47% a week before its results and still under board investigation, and Microsoft down 2.20%.

Trump says the war could end fairly soon, and Brent dips below $90

Trump said the war with Iran could end fairly soon and described Iran's military position as effectively over, after saying on Sunday that further attacks were highly unlikely while talks progress. Gulf states were reported to prefer keeping the US naval blockade of Iran in place. No outcome of Trump's Monday meeting with Qatar's prime minister was reported.

Crude kept drifting lower. WTI settled $86.04, down 0.93%, its lowest since early September, and Brent $91.49, down 1.17%, after briefly trading below $90 for the first time since August.

For this book the channel is the same, but weaker. Oil is falling, yet core inflation is running at 0.3% a month on shelter and services. Cheaper fuel alone will not stop a December hike.
