The taperanked by how unusual
Sigma (σ) measures a move against that instrument’s own recent behaviour, not against zero. One sigma is a normal day. Two sigma is a move bigger than roughly 95% of its last twenty sessions — rare enough to be information rather than noise. A 1.8% day on the S&P is ordinary in a volatile month and remarkable in a calm one; sigma is what tells the difference.

The war · the dayWTI $101.76 · Brent $115.81 · Mecca Pact in Tehran

Three capitals went to Tehran and nothing was agreed

The diplomacy is now multilateral, which is new. Top officials from Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan — the Mecca Pact — were due to meet Iran's leaders in Tehran on Monday, their first high-level contact since the Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia became the pressure point of the wider conflict. Separately Qatari mediators travelled to Tehran on Sunday in coordination with the United States. Nothing has been agreed on either track, and Iran told Pakistan's army chief there will be no talks with Washington until its demands are met. Every previous de-escalation headline in this conflict has been bilateral and has failed; four governments in the room is a different structure, and the oil market gave it a dollar and a half.

Meanwhile the attacks reached the pipeline that exists to bypass the Strait. The Houthis claimed strikes on pumping stations on Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline and on Riyadh airport over the weekend; Saudi Arabia has confirmed no damage and said on Sunday it had intercepted further missiles and drones. Three of that pipeline's pumping stations were hit in September and it restarted on 22 September. The East-West line is the route that moves Saudi crude to the Red Sea without passing Hormuz, so it is the single piece of infrastructure whose loss would make the chokepoint matter more rather than less. Nothing in Monday's reporting says flows have changed.

The price took the diplomacy and discounted the attacks. WTI settled $101.76, down $1.51 or 1.46%; Brent $115.81, down 91 cents, still near a six-month high after a week in which it gained 6%. That is the second consecutive decline from Thursday's peak, and it happened despite claimed strikes on an airport and a pipeline. A market that discounts physical attacks to price a meeting is a market that has decided the supply damage is already in the price — which is a defensible view at $116 Brent and a dangerous one if the meeting produces nothing.

And one structural detail with a long shadow. Saudi Arabia will begin storing crude oil in Singapore from the second half of 2027 to strengthen its regional supply-chain resilience. Read that as what it is: the world's largest exporter building inventory on the customer side of the Strait of Hormuz, with a timeline measured in years. Alongside a US Strategic Petroleum Reserve at its lowest level since 1982 and 603 American rigs unchanged for a second week, it says the people closest to this market are planning for the disruption to outlast the war rather than end with it. For this book the channel is unchanged: crude sets the inflation line, the inflation line sets the long end, and the long end sets what a 2028 cash flow is worth.
