What actually matteredone session, filtered

Peace talk sank oil, and the market rotated out of technology again

1. The first concrete opening from Tehran in weeks. Iran said its supreme leader had authorised peace talks and offered to reopen Hormuz under conditions, while Qatari mediators circulated a draft proposal. Crude fell more than 7% on words alone. Nothing has been signed, and Iran's conditions have not been published.

2. Technology fell while most stocks rose. Eight of eleven sectors and about two of every three stocks gained, but the Nasdaq fell 1.07% as chip and hardware names extended Friday's losses: Micron, Dell, Seagate and Western Digital each fell more than 5%. The equal-weight S&P rose 0.76% while the cap-weighted index fell 0.51%. The money is moving out of the largest names, not out of stocks.

3. Strong revenue was not enough for Palantir. Revenue beat its own guide by about 7% and US commercial passed government, yet the stock traded lower after hours. After Alphabet and Intel, the market is now asking more of AI results than a beat, and AMD reports on Tuesday.

The war · the dayWTI $96.46 · Brent $104.77

Iran says talks are authorised, and crude falls 7%

Iran said its supreme leader had granted the government authority for peace talks and that intensive exchanges with Washington were under way. It offered to reopen the Strait of Hormuz under strict conditions, and its negotiators continued meeting Qatari mediators over a draft proposal. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said over the weekend that Washington would prefer to end the conflict peacefully, and Trump said Iran wants to make a deal.

Oil gave back the whole of last week's attack premium and more. WTI settled $96.46, down 7.36%, and Brent $104.77, down 7.63%, after Friday's 2.08% gain on the limpet-mine attack. Energy stocks fell 3.96%. A deal would matter most for refined products, where the shortage has been worst.

The midterms are Tuesday. Trump has said Washington will not strike before the vote, and prediction markets price Democrats at about 94% to win the House and 74% the Senate. For this book the channel is the same: cheaper crude eases inflation, which eases the pressure on a Fed priced to hike in December.
