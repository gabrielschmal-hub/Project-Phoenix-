The week the market started auditing the AI bill — and the war stopped pretending
Five sessions, a third straight weekly gain, and a record close on Thursday: the S&P added 0.36% to 7,785.76 while the VIX fell to 14.25. Underneath that calm, two things changed. Investors began pricing how AI infrastructure is financed rather than what it might earn — Alphabet on Tuesday, Broadcom and Applied Materials on Friday — while the CPU side of the same trade got re-rated upward. And the war moved from rhetoric to enforcement: a US helicopter disabled a tanker running the blockade, Hormuz traffic sank to three-month lows, and the interim deal now expires on Tuesday.

Two inflation prints cooled, one consumer cracked, and the AI trade split in half

The market spent this week deciding that the cost of the build belongs in the valuation.TradingKey · Yahoo Finance · 11–14 Aug

The through-line is that this market has stopped paying for AI ambition and started pricing AI arithmetic — who is writing the cheques versus who is cashing them. You now hold one of each: Alphabet writing them, Intel cashing them.

The war · the weekkinetic enforcement · deal expires Tuesday

From rhetoric to Hellfire missiles in five sessions

STRIKEA US Navy helicopter disables the M/V Vela Nova with two Hellfire missiles in the Gulf of Oman. The first commercial vessel fired on since the ceasefire, and the moment the blockade stopped being a declaration. Engine room and steering gear, deliberately disabling rather than sinking.12 Aug
SHIPPINGHormuz traffic falls to near three-month lows as both sides claim control. Trump says the strait is open under US control; Iran says nothing passes without its permission. The traffic data settles it — whoever is right, the tankers are not sailing.13 Aug
MILITARYHegseth: the blockade can hold "indefinitely"; a relief carrier group sails Friday. Words backed by a rotation schedule. The market's hope that enforcement fades on its own was removed this week.13–14 Aug
OILWTI rose 4.7% on the week to 81.84 while equities made a record. Energy finished as the week's best sector. Crude is carrying the war risk that the equity indices are not.10–14 Aug

The week's arc runs from talks-are-close on Monday to a missile strike on Tuesday to annexation talk on Friday. Nothing was signed, and the June interim deal now expires on Tuesday 18 August with the blockade live and both sides demanding compensation from the other.
