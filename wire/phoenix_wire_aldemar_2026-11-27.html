Sigma (σ) measures a move against that instrument’s own recent behaviour, not against zero. One sigma is a normal day. Two sigma is a move bigger than roughly 95% of its last twenty sessions — rare enough to be information rather than noise.

What actually matteredone session, filtered

A holiday rally, and a supply scare in oil

1. The rebound held through the week. After a 4.7% jump on Monday, two small losses and a 1.6% gain on Friday, the S&P recovered about two-thirds of the previous week's 8.6% fall. Half-day volume is thin, so Friday's move is weaker evidence than Monday's.

2. Attacks on Saudi oil are the new risk. After Houthi claims against Ras Tanura on Wednesday, a strike on the Jazan refinery on Thursday pushed Brent to its first weekly gain this month. WTI barely moved, and the gap between the two is now about $12.

3. The Fed has not changed. A December hike is still close to fully priced after Wednesday's firm inflation data. A rally in stocks does not change that.

A strike on Jazan, and OPEC+ meets on Sunday

A projectile hit Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery on Thursday, killing a worker and halting some operations, according to the Saudi energy ministry, which blamed the Houthis. Iran's attacks on ships passing through Hormuz continued during the week, and the US blockade of Iranian ports remains in place.

OPEC+ meets on Sunday. People familiar with the talks say the group will keep first-quarter output targets unchanged, while Iraq is seeking a higher production limit.

Brent settled $95.44, up about 3% for the week, and January WTI $83.08. Oil is still down sharply for November.
