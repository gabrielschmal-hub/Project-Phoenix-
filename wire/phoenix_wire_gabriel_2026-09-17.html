The taperanked by how unusual
Sigma (σ) measures a move against that instrument’s own recent behaviour, not against zero. One sigma is a normal day. Two sigma is a move bigger than roughly 95% of its last twenty sessions — rare enough to be information rather than noise. A 1.8% day on the S&P is ordinary in a volatile month and remarkable in a calm one; sigma is what tells the difference.

What actually matteredone session, filtered

A hawkish hike bought credibility, and the bond market paid for it the next day

1. The curve rallied after a hawkish hike, which is the opposite of the textbook. The ten-year fell 5bp to 4.94% and went back under five percent the session after the Fed raised rates and published a dot plot showing twelve of eighteen members wanting another. That is a bond market concluding that a central bank willing to tighten now — into political pressure, unanimously, with unusually blunt language — is less likely to have to tighten far more later. Warsh spent credibility on Wednesday and was repaid on Thursday.

2. The growth scare lasted one session. Wednesday's split — Dow down 1.15%, Nasdaq flat — read as the market marking down the economy. Thursday ran it exactly backwards, Nasdaq +1.67% against the Dow's +0.71%. One session of cyclicals lagging is rotation; this brief said three would be a growth scare. We are back to one, and the count resets. Keep watching it: this book has almost no cyclical exposure, so a genuine growth scare would hurt it late rather than early, which is the worst way to find out.

3. The yen has not been unwinding into the decision, and that is the risk in today. USD/JPY sat near 156 going into the Bank of Japan — weaker yen, not stronger — so the carry trade is fully on when the announcement lands. A hike to 1.25% is all but fully priced and would be Japan's highest rate in about 31 years, but ING has warned the market may be overestimating the BoJ's hawkishness. Both outcomes move things: a hawkish hike forces the unwind everyone has been waiting for, and a dovish one sends the yen lower still and rewards the crowded trade one more time.

The war · the daycrude's third straight fall · repairs on schedule

The supply shock is deflating on schedule, and nothing has replaced it

Crude fell a third consecutive session, 1.16% to about $101.25, continuing the retreat that began when Saudi Aramco said it could bypass the damaged section of the East-West pipeline and restore roughly half its capacity within days, with full operations in about six weeks. Aramco is also moving barrels to Asian refiners ship-to-ship near Oman's Sohar port, routing around the damage rather than waiting on the repair. From $104.10 on Tuesday to $101.25 on Thursday is not a collapse, but it is a trend, and it is the first one going the right way for inflation since the line was cut.

The strait is unchanged: Hormuz flows remain near 6-9m bpd against more than a fifth of world supply in peacetime, so the structural problem is still there. What has gone is the panic premium. The dot plot published on Wednesday was written while crude was still rising — if this retreat holds into the October CPI, the median dot is the first thing that has to move, and the next decision is 28 October.

The size is the point. Citadel Securities put roughly $6.2 trillion of US options exposure expiring today — about 23% of all outstanding exposure, and possibly more by settlement. It does not clear in one moment: standard index options settle on the opening quotation, PM-settled contracts in the afternoon, single stocks separately. So the pressure is spread across the session rather than concentrated at the bell, and the direction is set by where the index sits relative to heavily populated strikes rather than by any view.

The Bank of Japan lands before New York opens, which is the part that matters for the expiry. A move in the yen or in JGB yields spills into global rates and US equity futures overnight, and can shift the index toward or away from those strikes before a single share trades here. Two events that would each be significant alone are arriving in an order that lets the first one steer the second.

No name in this book reports before 28 October. Alphabet 28 October, Apple 29 October, Palantir 2 November; Okta already reported. The only company event on any holding is Oktane on 23 September. Six weeks without earnings risk — and nothing coming to rescue a position with a number either.

Three things to check first tomorrow. Whether the yen strengthened or weakened on the decision, because a dovish hike is the outcome nobody is hedged for. Whether the ten-year stayed below five percent once the expiry cleared. And whether any position was taken out intraday — a triple witching can pierce a stop on flow alone and close nowhere near it, which is exactly the failure mode that hid an exit here eight days ago.
