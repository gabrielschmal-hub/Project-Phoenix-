The taperanked by how unusual
Sigma (σ) measures a move against that instrument’s own recent behaviour, not against zero. One sigma is a normal day. Two sigma is a move bigger than roughly 95% of its last twenty sessions — rare enough to be information rather than noise. A 1.8% day on the S&P is ordinary in a volatile month and remarkable in a calm one; sigma is what tells the difference.

The war · the dayHormuz crude ~10.3m b/d · WTI $101.79

Crude is flowing again. Diesel is not.

The recovery in crude is real and the number is specific. Kpler puts crude flows through the Strait of Hormuz at about 10.3m barrels a day on a seven-day average to Saturday, which it calls 76% of pre-war levels; US energy secretary Chris Wright put Gulf flows at about 80%. That is a chokepoint functioning at roughly four-fifths of capacity while being actively mined. Kpler counts at least 18 vessels attacked in the past fortnight, and on Tuesday Iran's Revolutionary Guards said two tankers were set ablaze after hitting mines in the Strait. Both things are true at once, and the market has decided the flow number is the one that prices the barrel.

The damage has moved from crude to refined product, and that is a different inflation problem. Refined products are now 11% of cargoes through the Strait, down from more than 20% before the conflict. Middle East refinery throughput was 7.3m barrels a day in August against 9.9m in February — a quarter of the region's refining gone. Crude can be re-routed; a cracking margin cannot. That shows up as diesel and jet fuel rather than as gasoline, which means it reaches the consumer through freight and airfares instead of the pump — slower, stickier, and harder for a central bank to dismiss as a supply shock that will pass.

Tuesday's price action was a diplomatic headline reversing a five-month high. Brent ran to $111.99 and settled $108.79, down 76 cents. WTI's November contract expired, settling $101.79, down $1.87 or 1.8%, having touched $110.48 — the highest for a nearby contract since April. The turn came when Iran's president Masoud Pezeshkian received Pakistan's army chief Field Marshal Asim Munir in Tehran with a new mediation proposal, per Nournews. Pakistan brokered an interim agreement in June which faltered within weeks, and a diplomat involved said reports of an imminent deal were inaccurate and that talks are ongoing. So the whole of a three-dollar reversal rests on a meeting, not an agreement.

The rest of the Gulf picture, unverified but relevant. Houthi forces claimed strikes on an Aramco site at Yanbu, the Shaybah oilfield and airports at Riyadh and Abha; Saudi authorities did not confirm them and they have not been independently verified. A Houthi attack on a government security training camp in Yemen's Shabwa province reportedly killed 58 people. On the inventory side, a Reuters poll expects US crude stocks fell about 800,000 barrels in the week to 16 October, with the EIA report due Wednesday. For this book the transmission is unchanged: energy has pushed the thirty-year mortgage to 7.40%, its highest in nearly three years, and that is now visibly breaking housing starts.
