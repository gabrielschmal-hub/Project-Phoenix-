What actually matteredone session, filtered

Peace hopes and a chip rebound, with CPI the next test

1. Trump's signal on Iran moved oil and stocks together. Saying further attacks were highly unlikely while talks progress took WTI down 4.38% and Brent 5.85%, and lifted airlines, consumer stocks and small caps. WTI is now back to its early-September level.

2. The chip trade split by company. AMD rose 13% on an upgrade that expects its data-centre revenue to grow about 135% by 2027, and equipment makers rose more than 10%. Nvidia fell 4% on its board investigation, a week before its own results on 17 November. Thursday's sector-wide data-centre fear has become a stock-by-stock question.

3. The Fed debate moves to CPI. Hike odds rose to 84% and the two-year rose 5bp. Tuesday's October CPI will have no official annual rate because of last year's data gap, so the monthly figures carry the weight: about 0.3% headline and 0.2% core are expected.

Trump signals restraint, and oil falls to its lowest since early September

On Sunday Trump said he was highly unlikely to order further attacks on Iran as long as talks to end the conflict progressed, and he was due to meet Qatar's prime minister, the main mediator, on Monday. Reuters noted no sign of a large-scale US operation. Shipping attacks around the Strait of Hormuz continued.

Crude fell hard and kept falling after the settle. WTI settled $86.85, down 4.38%, and Brent $92.57, down 5.85%; Brent's loss widened to more than $7 after the settle. JPMorgan estimated an oil surplus of 1.1m barrels a day in September and 1.4m in October.

For this book the channel is the same. Cheaper crude eases inflation, but the Fed is priced to hike in December anyway, and Tuesday's CPI will show how much of October's oil spike reached prices.
