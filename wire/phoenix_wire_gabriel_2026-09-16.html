The taperanked by how unusual
Sigma (σ) measures a move against that instrument’s own recent behaviour, not against zero. One sigma is a normal day. Two sigma is a move bigger than roughly 95% of its last twenty sessions — rare enough to be information rather than noise. A 1.8% day on the S&P is ordinary in a volatile month and remarkable in a calm one; sigma is what tells the difference.

A third straight session with nothing above two sigma — but the Dow came closest, and the spread is the story. Its 1.15% fall was 1.58σ, against 0.81σ for the S&P's 0.44% and 0.04σ for a Nasdaq that finished flat. A Fed that hiked and promised more should hurt long-duration growth first. It did the opposite: the selling was in cyclicals, industrials and banks — the part of the index that needs an economy rather than a multiple. Read that as the market marking down growth in 2027, not the discount rate.

The decision. Twenty-five basis points to 3.75-4.00%, unanimous, the first increase since 2023 — and the statement dropped the three dissents that had preferred a hike last time, because it gave them one. The committee described activity as expanding at a solid pace with productivity growth strong and capital investment robust, and said the action will support a timelier return to the committee's 2 percent goal. The projections carry a 2026 median implying an average rate of about 4.125%.

What Warsh actually said. That we must be confident that underlying inflation is moving to our objective, clearly and at sufficient speed; that the Fed's predominant focus is on the price stability side; and that this summer's inflation readings do not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully improved. Asked about the bond market he named three causes for higher yields — a stronger economy, a surge in capital investment competing for capital, and geopolitics — and he defended Fed independence. None of that is a put. The ten-year held above 5%.

Breadth and the rest of the board. Roughly 61% of issues declined. The Russell fell 0.43% and gold 0.61% to about $4,294 — bullion lower again while the curve sits at five percent, the same real-yields-outrank-everything signal as Tuesday. Bitcoin was firmer. Crude fell 1.59% to around $102.40, its first decline since the pipeline was shut.

Positioning. The decision is behind us and the calendar is not: the Bank of Japan decides Friday and triple witching settles the same session. Dealer gamma is still negative, so a market that has just been told to expect another hike will amplify rather than absorb whatever Friday produces.

What actually matteredone session, filtered

A hawkish Fed hit the economy trade, not the duration trade — and the one-story book did not move as one story

1. The market repriced growth, not the discount rate. A central bank that hikes and signals another should punish long-duration assets hardest. Instead the Dow fell 1.15%, the S&P 0.44% and the Nasdaq nothing at all, with 61% of issues lower. Cyclicals, industrials and banks took the damage — the parts of the index that need an economy rather than a multiple. Warsh saying the economy has strengthened while promising to hold rates higher is, to those businesses, a promise to slow them down. This book has almost no exposure to that trade, which is why a hawkish surprise cost it nothing.

2. The narrative concentration flagged on Monday did not hold, and that is useful. GOOG, OKTA were supposed to move together because all three ride the AI-safety story. On Wednesday PLTR rose 1.03%, OKTA fell 1.22% and GOOG fell 0.61%. The correlation is real on story-days and absent otherwise — so the risk is not that these names are permanently joined, it is that they re-couple precisely when the story moves, which is the moment it matters. Treat it as a conditional exposure, not a standing one.

3. Oil broke its trend and almost nobody noticed. Aramco can bypass the damaged section and restore about half the East-West pipeline within days, with full capacity in roughly six weeks, and is moving crude to Asian refiners ship-to-ship near Oman's Sohar port. US commercial inventories fell only 640,000 barrels against an expected 1.6m draw. Crude's first down day since the shutdown removes the input that carried August CPI to 3.4% and hike odds from 70% to above 90%. If crude keeps falling, the case for the next hike weakens — and the dot plot was written before this news.

The war · the dayAramco restores half the line · crude's first fall

The supply shock got a repair timeline, and the price responded immediately

Saudi Aramco said it can bypass the damaged section of the East-West pipeline and bring roughly half its capacity back online within days, with full operations in about six weeks — four days after drones traced to Maysan province in south-eastern Iraq forced the shutdown. It is also increasing shipments to Asian refiners through ship-to-ship transfers near Oman's Sohar port, routing around the damage rather than waiting for it to be fixed.

Crude fell 1.59% to about $102.40, its first decline since the line was cut, helped by US commercial inventories falling just 640,000 barrels against an expected 1.6m draw — a build in disguise. The strait itself is unchanged: Hormuz flows are still down to roughly 6-9m bpd from over a fifth of world supply. What changed is that the bypass now has a date, and a supply shock with a date is a price move rather than a regime.
