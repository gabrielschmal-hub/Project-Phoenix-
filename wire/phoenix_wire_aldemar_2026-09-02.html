One strike becomes a campaign — crude $90, the 10-year at a 20-month high
At noon New York time on Tuesday, US forces began striking IRGC targets across Iran — explosions were reported on Qeshm Island and at Bandar Abbas, Chabahar, Jask and Sirik. CENTCOM tied it to "recent attempted attacks by the IRGC against commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and against American service members", after two Saudi supertankers were hit by projectiles late Monday. This is no longer Sunday's single retaliatory action; it is a campaign. WTI settled +5.20% at 90.22 and Brent +4.60% at 94.65 after trading above $95, the 10-year went to 4.796% — the highest since January 2025 — and September hike odds moved to roughly two-thirds. The S&P fell 0.71% to 7,631.58, the Nasdaq 1.03%, the Russell 1.23%. The book took it well: AAPL rose 2.61% on John Ternus's first day as chief executive, the largest single-name move you hold and the only one that mattered.

The war · US–Irancampaign · strikes across five Iranian sites · two supertankers hit · Brent above $95 intraday

The US stops retaliating and starts a campaign

STRIKEUS forces begin striking IRGC targets inside Iran at noon ET — explosions on Qeshm Island and at Bandar Abbas, Chabahar, Jask and Sirik. CENTCOM announced it on X while the strikes were under way: "U.S. forces began striking Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) targets in Iran. The strikes follow recent attempted attacks by the IRGC against commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and against American service members deployed to the region." Sunday's action at Larak was two launchers and a stated "imminent threat". This is five locations across the length of the coast, and the framing has moved from prevention to punishment.CENTCOM via Navy Times · Defense News · 2026-09-01
SHIPPINGTwo Saudi supertankers struck by projectiles late Monday; UKMTO reports a tanker hit by three projectiles 17nm east of Khasab, Oman. No casualties and no environmental damage reported. The IRGC separately claimed a tanker had struck two naval mines in the southern strait and caught fire; the US military called that disinformation. Whichever account is true, the insurable fact is the same — outbound crude carriers are being hit, which is what puts a premium in the price rather than a headline.UKMTO via RFE/RL · Navy Times · 2026-08-31
THREATParliament Speaker Qalibaf: "If the enemy wants us not to export oil from the Persian Gulf, no one will be able to export oil". The IRGC spokesman said the US would "regret its new attacks"; Trump said "we're going to hit them hard" and called Iran a "failed nation" with 350% inflation. Qalibaf's sentence is the one that matters, because it names the tail: not harassment of shipping but closure of the strait. Nothing in Tuesday's tape prices that — Brent at $95 is a risk premium, not a closure.RFE/RL · Navy Times · 2026-09-01
DIPLOMACYPezeshkian: if the US returns to its June memorandum commitments, Iran "will immediately reciprocate". The June MoU deferred the hard questions and the 60-day talks that followed ended without agreement, which is how a sanctions standoff became a shooting one. But the offer restated on Tuesday is specific and includes free navigation through the strait. An off-ramp that both sides can still name is worth more than the day's headlines suggest.Just Security · RFE/RL · 2026-09-01

The transmission is strait → oil → inflation → the Fed, and every link fired on Tuesday: crude +5.2%, the 10-year to a 20-month high, hike odds to two-thirds. Notice what did not happen — gold fell 1.9% and the VIX only reached 16.34. This was not a fear bid, it was a rates repricing, which tells you the market is treating Iran as an inflation problem rather than a solvency one. That framing holds until the strait actually closes. Two scheduled numbers now matter more than the next headline out of Bandar Abbas: the employment report on Friday and CPI on 11 September, both landing before the FOMC on the 16th.
