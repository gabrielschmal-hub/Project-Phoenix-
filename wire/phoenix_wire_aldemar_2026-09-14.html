The war · the dayBrent above $105 · pipeline shut five to six weeks

The pipeline built to survive a closed Hormuz was shut by drones from Iraq

Saudi Arabia suspended the East-West pipeline on 13 September after a drone strike the kingdom traced to Maysan province in south-eastern Iraq, near the Iranian border. The 1,200km line normally carries 4–5m barrels a day against a 7m maximum, running crude west to Yanbu on the Red Sea. It exists precisely to bypass the Strait of Hormuz — and Hormuz flows are already down to roughly 6–9m bpd from more than a fifth of global supply in peacetime. Repairs are estimated at five to six weeks.

Both the chokepoint and the alternative built for exactly this scenario are now impaired at the same time. Brent went above $105 and was quoted near $108; WTI closed $102.30. The transmission into this book is direct and already working: crude into the CPI print, the CPI print into Wednesday's decision. Petrol added 3.9% in August and took the headline number with it.
