The transmission is oil → inflation → the regime call, and Monday partially cut the first link: with crude down 2.4% and the strait's closure already priced, the marginal sanction now hurts Tehran more than it hurts the CPI path. That is quietly good for the book's macro backdrop — CPI y/y at 3.3 against the 2.5 floor gets a little breathing room if crude stays at 85 rather than 93. The risk that replaces it is binary rather than gradual: Rezaei's threat, or a strike on an enforcing party, would move oil in a single session rather than a week. In negative gamma, that kind of headline lands on a tape with nothing damping it.
