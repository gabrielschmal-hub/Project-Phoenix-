Hot inflation and tariffs knock stocks down — and a late rally pulls them most of the way back
Core PCE rose 3.3%, above forecast, GDP was revised up to 4.3%, and new tariffs of up to 20% were proposed. The Nasdaq was down as much as 4.6% before closing 0.62% lower; the S&P fell 0.25% and the Dow rose 0.33%. Nvidia fell 3.91% and WTI rose about 2%. The book is flat; Q4 is +45.38 realised, heat 0R.
In five minutesthree things
1A deep morning sell-off, then a late recovery. The Nasdaq was down as much as 4.6% and the Dow more than 1,100 points, and the Nasdaq-100 touched 20% below its October peak. By the close the Nasdaq was down 0.62%, the S&P 0.25%, while the Dow rose 0.33% and the Russell 0.78%. Late in the day the Wall Street Journal reported that officials had seen no evidence that Chinese intelligence directed a tech executive to spy on Jensen Huang, and tech losses narrowed.
2Inflation stayed hot and the economy strong. October core PCE rose 3.3% from a year earlier, against 3.2% expected, with a third straight 0.3% monthly rise; headline PCE was 3.8%. Third-quarter growth was revised up to 4.3%, and jobless claims fell to 189,000. A December hike is about 97% priced, and futures put the odds of another in January at about 63%.
3New tariffs were proposed. The administration proposed duties on 14 trading partners after a Section 301 investigation into excess industrial capacity, up to 20% on China and 15% on the EU, Japan and South Korea, with chips, autos and metals excluded. They are not expected before January. Nvidia fell 3.91% to 149.30, after being down more than 9%. January WTI settled at $83.68, up about 2%.

The book is still flat. Palantir rose 1.50% to 163.59, about 16% below the 194.25 exit, and AMZN fell 3.11% to 244.43, back below the 250.28 exit. Q4 stays at +45.38, +2.77R, all realised, and the day was +0.00. The full 3.0R is available.

What actually matteredone session, filtered

Firm inflation, tariffs, and an AI trade that keeps swinging

1. The inflation data backs a December hike. Core PCE above forecast, GDP revised up to 4.3% and claims near historic lows leave the Fed little reason to pause. Markets have priced December and are starting to price January.

2. Tariffs are back as a risk. The proposed duties are not due before January, and the US-China truce runs to 10 January. They add to inflation risk at a time when the Fed is already hiking.

3. AI stocks are swinging on headlines. Nvidia rose 31% on Monday and has fallen about 10% since. A Nasdaq-100 that touched 20% below its peak, then recovered, shows how quickly sentiment is changing. The broad market held up better: most stocks rose.
