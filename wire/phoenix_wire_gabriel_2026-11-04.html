What actually matteredone session, filtered

A Democratic Congress, a cheaper barrel, and a split inside the AI trade

1. The market liked the result. A Democratic House and Senate with a Republican president means little new legislation in either direction. Stocks rose broadly, with the equal-weight S&P up 1.86%, and the Dow had its fifth-largest point gain ever. Health care and consumer stocks led; Trump Media fell about 24%.

2. Oil is now pricing an end to the war. Vance blamed the war for the losses, and traders bet the pressure to end it will rise. WTI has fallen from $104.12 on Friday to $88.12 in three sessions, about 15%. That is the fastest relief on inflation this autumn, and yields and the dollar fell with it.

3. AI split into buyers and builders. The platforms rose, with Amazon, Microsoft and Alphabet up between 3% and 7%, while the chip index fell 3.13% and data-centre suppliers fell 10% or more on fears of tighter limits under a Democratic Congress. After the close AppLovin rose about 23% on revenue up 55%, and Qualcomm fell about 6% on a 26% drop in phone-chip sales.

The war · the dayWTI $88.12 · Brent $94.62

The vote raises the pressure to end the war, and crude falls 8%

Vance blamed the war for Republican losses, and oil traders read the result as more pressure on Washington to settle. Qatar, which is mediating, said it expects feedback within 36 hours. Iran called the US reply to its Hormuz plan unconvincing, as Trump confirmed Tuesday's strikes on Qeshm Island.

Crude gave up the whole war premium of October. WTI settled $88.12, down 8.29%, and Brent $94.62, down 8.67%, both at two-month lows. Energy stocks fell 4.74%. Nothing has been signed and the strait has not reopened; the price assumes a deal that does not yet exist.

For this book the channel is the same, pointing the other way. Cheaper crude eases inflation and pulled yields lower, but a December hike is still priced at about 77%, because the Fed is reacting to inflation already in the data.
