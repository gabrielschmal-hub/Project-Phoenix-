The worst week for the S&P since April 2025. The Dow fell 8.02% at 3.52σ, the S&P 8.61% at 3.41σ, the Russell 7.48% at 3.31σ and the Nasdaq 10.02% at 3.23σ. Gold fell 8.93% at 2.46σ and WTI 4.70% at 0.84σ, measured on the January contract after December expired on Friday.

The indices, precisely. The S&P fell 651.28 points to 6,910.39, the Nasdaq 2,518.54 to 22,617.73, the Dow 4,200.22 to 48,147.33 and the Russell 208.70 to 2,581.79. The S&P fell on all five days. Spot gold fell from $3,881.75 to $3,535.00, and the VIX ended at 35.89, its highest close since late March.

Five sessions. Monday: the S&P −1.92% as chips sold off on new rules for Chinese access to US chips. Tuesday: the S&P −1.03% to a five-month low with every sector down; after the close Nvidia beat, with $115.8bn of revenue and a $130bn guide. Wednesday: Nvidia reversed to −2.89%, the Fed minutes leaned toward more hikes and Waller called for a half-point; gold and oil each fell more than 3%. Thursday: the S&P −2.02% for a sixth straight loss and the Dow below 50,000; Walmart beat and fell; Trump, Xi and Putin pledged more fuel supply. Friday: Nvidia −14.01% on a reported SEC accounting inquiry, the Nasdaq −5.28% and the S&P −3.70%.

Bonds turned into a haven late. The two-year held near 4.80% for most of the week, then fell about 13bp on Friday to 4.63%; the ten-year ended near 4.92%. December hike pricing barely moved, at about 90%.

What actually matteredfive sessions, filtered

Nvidia's crisis became the market's, and the Fed offered no help

1. Strong numbers did not save Nvidia. It beat revenue forecasts by about $6.7bn and guided $5bn above consensus, then fell every day that followed, ending the week at 125.53 after a 14% fall on Friday. A new CEO search, a reported SEC accounting inquiry, tighter export rules and Trump's threat of penalties on AI data centres abroad outweighed the results. Chip makers fell 12-14% on Friday alone.

2. The Fed is still leaning toward hikes. The minutes showed almost all officials expect more increases, Waller called for a half-point and Cook warned against falling behind the curve. A December hike stayed about 90% priced even as stocks fell 8.6%. Only on Friday did bonds rally hard.

3. Selling spread to everything. Gold fell 8.93% over the week and silver more, bitcoin fell below $60,000, and more than 90% of S&P stocks fell on Thursday. Good earnings from Walmart and Nvidia were sold. That pattern points to investors cutting risk across the board rather than picking winners.

The war · the weekWTI (Jan) $87.74 → $83.62

Attacks, a fuel pledge, and a third weekly fall for crude

The fighting continued at a steady pace. Drones were fired at a US radar in Bahrain, Iran targeted a US air base in Kuwait after a US strike on Qeshm Island, and tanker attacks around Hormuz ran at roughly one a day. Iran reported mine strikes on tankers that could not be verified.

The diplomacy produced a pledge, not a deal. Trump, Xi and Putin said in Shenzhen that their countries would increase supplies of diesel and gasoline, with no targets or dates.

Crude fell 4.70% on the week on the January basis, as US stocks built and the IEA's surplus outlook outweighed the attacks. For this book the channel is the same: crude feeds inflation, and the Fed is still focused on it.
