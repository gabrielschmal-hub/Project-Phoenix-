The taperanked by how unusual
Sigma (σ) measures a move against that instrument’s own recent behaviour, not against zero. One sigma is a normal day. Two sigma is a move bigger than roughly 95% of its last twenty sessions — rare enough to be information rather than noise. A 1.8% day on the S&P is ordinary in a volatile month and remarkable in a calm one; sigma is what tells the difference.

What actually matteredone session, filtered

The oil shock is over, and the projection that justified another hike was written before it ended

1. Three weeks of supply panic unwound in six sessions. WTI went $93 to $104 when the East-West pipeline was cut on 13 September, and is now $89.42 — below where it started. Three things did it, in order: Aramco's plan to bypass the damaged section and restore half the line within days; satellite evidence that Saudi Arabia is moving 2.8m barrels a day through Hormuz against 700,000 in August; and now Tehran offering to reopen the strait outright. A supply shock with a repair date is a price move. A supply shock the adversary offers to end is not a shock at all.

2. That leaves the Fed's own projection stranded. Twelve of eighteen members signed a dot plot on 16 September implying another hike this year, with crude above $102 and a 3.4% CPI print driven substantially by a 3.9% monthly rise in petrol. Crude is 14% lower a week later. The October CPI will not capture all of it and the committee does not meet until 28 October — but if energy keeps falling, the hawkish path that has set the tone for this entire month becomes the thing most likely to be revised. This book is long duration through technology, so that revision would help it.

The war · the dayIran offers to reopen Hormuz in seven days

Tehran put a timeline on the strait, and crude lost nine percent

A senior Iranian official said Tehran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days if Washington eases military pressure and lifts its blockade of Iranian ports, after which talks would resume on a permanent settlement. The offer reached Washington through mediators six days earlier. There is a possibility of moving toward an agreement, the official said, while insisting the US show seriousness and commitment.

Secretary of State Rubio said the administration was open to that, seeking discussions that could also cover Iran's nuclear programme. But a meeting between Presidents Trump and Pezeshkian has been ruled out — which qualifies Monday's reading that the two might meet at the General Assembly. Pezeshkian addresses the Assembly today and will instead meet foreign leaders, US think-tank representatives and intermediary states.

The market took the offer at close to face value: WTI fell 8.6% to $89.42, a two-week low, and gold gave back 0.78% as the geopolitical premium came out of both. Hormuz still carries more than a fifth of world oil in peacetime, so a reopening is the single largest supply event available to this market — and it is now being negotiated rather than fought over. The risk in that is symmetrical: an offer conditional on American concessions is also an offer that can be withdrawn.
