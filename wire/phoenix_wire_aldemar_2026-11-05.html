What actually matteredone session, filtered

The election turned into a data-centre problem overnight

1. The market re-read the election result. On Wednesday it saw gridlock and bought. On Thursday it focused on what a Democratic Congress and state votes in Texas and Georgia could mean for data-centre buildout, and UBS put about 30% of planned 2028 additions at risk. The companies that sell into that buildout fell 10-20% in a day.

2. It spread well beyond chips. All eleven sectors fell, the Russell fell 4.45% and only about one S&P stock in seven rose. Technology was down 8%, but industrials, which include power and cooling suppliers to data centres, fell 4.12%. The VIX rose almost 70%.

3. The Fed backdrop got easier at the same time. Productivity beat, labour costs fell, and the two-year yield fell about 12bp. December hike odds dropped from above 80% to about two-thirds. That did nothing for stocks on Thursday, but Friday's payrolls now carry more weight for the December decision.

The war · the dayWTI $90.52 · Brent $98.29

Deal hopes fade a little, and crude bounces

Oil rebounded as the diplomacy looked less certain. Eurasia Group cut its odds of a US-Iran deal and raised its odds of a continued standoff. Tanker attacks continued near the Strait of Hormuz, and Saudi Arabia issued alerts around Yanbu as Houthi attacks went on. Qatar's expected feedback on the talks had not been reported by the close.

The bounce was small next to the fall. WTI settled $90.52, up 2.72%, and Brent $98.29, up 3.88%, against falls of 8.29% and 8.67% on Wednesday. WTI is still about 13% below Friday's $104.12.

For this book the channel is the same. Crude feeds inflation, and inflation feeds the Fed. With oil well off its highs and labour costs falling, the December hike is less certain than it was a week ago.
