In five minutesthree things
1Chips gave back much of Monday's jump. The Philadelphia semiconductor index fell 7.88% a day after rising 14.03%, and Nvidia fell 5.86% to 155.38. Micron fell 10.97%, AMD, Intel and Applied Materials 7-9%, and Analog Devices 17.37% after its results. Reports cited doubts about returns on AI spending and reports of new Trump tariffs on 16 trading partners. Anthropic closed at $81.91, below its $83 offer price.
2Money moved into defensive and value stocks. The Dow rose 0.85% to 50,018.70, back above 50,000, and the Russell rose 0.57%, while the Nasdaq fell 1.48% and the S&P 0.50%. Health care rose 2.88%, led by Lilly, Novo Nordisk, J&J and Merck, and Sanofi agreed to buy Insmed for $41bn in cash. Kohl's rose 19.48% and Dollar Tree 11.18% on results.
3Consumers sounded much happier. Conference Board confidence jumped to 103.3 from 91.9, against about 92.2 expected, the highest since January 2025, and fewer people said jobs were hard to get. One-year inflation expectations eased to 5.7%. A December hike is still close to fully priced, and PCE inflation and Q3 GDP come on Wednesday. Spot gold fell 2.15%.

The book is still flat. Palantir rose 1.14% to 161.18, about 17% below the 194.25 exit, and AMZN fell 1.21% to 252.29. Q4 stays at +45.38, +2.77R, all realised, and the day was +0.00. The full 3.0R is available.

Sigma (σ) measures a move against that instrument’s own recent behaviour, not against zero. One sigma is a normal day. Two sigma is a move bigger than roughly 95% of its last twenty sessions — rare enough to be information rather than noise.

What actually matteredone session, filtered

The AI rebound did not hold, but the rest of the market did

1. Chips gave back half of Monday. The semiconductor index fell 7.88% after a 14.03% rise. Huang's return answered one question, but doubts about AI spending, the SEC inquiry and tariff reports are still there. Anthropic trading below its offer price added to the caution.

2. The selling stayed in one corner. The Dow and the Russell rose, and health care, staples and retailers gained. That is rotation, not a broad sell-off like last week, which is a better sign for the market as a whole.

3. Strong confidence does not help the Fed case. A jump in consumer confidence and better job perceptions give the Fed less reason to hold off. Wednesday's PCE report is the last big number before the December meeting.

The war · the dayWTI (Jan) ~$82.05

More ship attacks in Hormuz, and crude bounces

Attacks on shipping continued. UKMTO said three vessels were hit by unidentified projectiles in the strait on Monday, with all crew accounted for, and Iran's Revolutionary Guards said they targeted a container ship. Iran also claimed mine strikes on three tankers, which could not be confirmed.

Washington is looking at cyberattacks too. The Wall Street Journal reported that the US is investigating whether Iran was behind cyberattacks on as many as 50 energy tankers; an administration official said no evidence of Iranian involvement had been found.

Crude rose but stays near three-month lows. January WTI finished near $82.05, up about 2.3%. Deutsche Bank estimates the market is oversupplied by 1.7m barrels a day, and OPEC+ meets on 29 November.
