The taperanked by how unusual
Sigma (σ) measures a move against that instrument’s own recent behaviour, not against zero. One sigma is a normal day. Two sigma is a move bigger than roughly 95% of its last twenty sessions — rare enough to be information rather than noise. A 1.8% day on the S&P is ordinary in a volatile month and remarkable in a calm one; sigma is what tells the difference.

The war · the dayWTI $97.97 · the spread collapsed to $7.95

The two benchmarks split three dollars apart in a single session

WTI settled $97.97, up $2.88 or 3.03%, on a sharp decline in US crude inventories. Brent settled $105.92, down 14 cents, as traders weighed a possible United States easing of restrictions on the 'shadow fleet' of tankers — the vessels moving sanctioned crude — against the standing Middle East premium. The spread between the two collapsed to $7.95 from $10.97, a three-dollar narrowing in one day and the tightest it has been in this stretch.

That split is worth more attention than either number alone. An American inventory draw tightening WTI while a prospective sanctions easing loosens Brent is the two halves of the oil market being priced by different news for the first time since the conflict began. If the shadow-fleet easing is real it puts sanctioned barrels into the seaborne market, which is Brent's problem and not WTI's. If the US draw persists, the American barrel keeps tightening into a refining system that is already the constraint.

And the refining constraint is the part that reaches this book. The IEA's figure from Wednesday still stands: combined net gasoil exports from the Middle East and Russia fell 1.3m barrels a day in September to 820,000, down more than 60% year on year, with no stock rebuild expected before the second half of 2027. Gulf crude exports are back at 13.9m barrels a day, over 75% of pre-war, while refined product exports are 1.9m against 4.7m before the war. Crude flows; product does not. That gap is why gasoline rose 20.3% at the producer level in September and did over half of the CPI increase — and why it then showed up as a retail-sales beat that was really a fuel bill.

So the war is now the transmission mechanism and not the headline. Nothing was struck on Thursday. The missile that hit Aramco's Dhahran command centre on Tuesday moved crude about a dollar. What moves this book is the second-order effect: energy into the price indices, price indices into the rate expectation, rate expectation into the multiple on three long-duration growth names. Crude at $97.97 is three dollars higher than when the week began, and the October CPI lands 10 November with that embedded in it.
