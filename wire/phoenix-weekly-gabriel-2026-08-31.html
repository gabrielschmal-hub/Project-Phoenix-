Nvidia answered the AI financing question with $96.2bn of revenue and a $108bn guide, ending the audit that had run all August. Then on Friday the Fed chair walked into Jackson Hole and doubled September hike odds to 57%. The book ends the week with one closed trade, both BAC positions recovered off Thursday's break, two new names added — PLTR on Thursday and AAPL on Friday, both still provisional — and a new question: rates, not war.

NOISEFiled and ignored: analyst target trims tracking Intel's share count, Nvidia pre-earnings prediction pieces, chart-pattern notes. A week of commentary that followed prices rather than led them. None of it changed a thesis, a level or a size.various · 24–28 Aug

The book's ledger for the week: Aldemar's INTC exited on the 24th at 86.26 for −0.79R, on a stop that had been raised from 81.10 to 86.26 mid-drawdown — two sessions before Nvidia's print lifted the sector, with INTC now trading above that exit. That is the week's real lesson, and it is not that the exit was wrong: a stop honoured is always right. It is that tightening a stop mid-trade converts a wide-stop thesis position into a tight-stop tactical one, and the trade you end up in is not the trade you sized. Gabriel's INTC also closed cleanly at −1.00R on the 18th. Both accounts now carry BAC into a Fed meeting, and Gabriel holds GOOG at +0.84R. Two positions were also opened this week and both books now hold them: PLTR around 184 into Thursday's breakout and AAPL around 321 into Friday's strength. Neither has a stop or target recorded yet, which is the one piece of housekeeping worth doing before Monday's open — every read in this brief is built on the distance between price and a level you chose in advance, and two of the five positions currently have no such level.

The week the pressure campaign and the peace track ran at the same time

POLITICSMonday: Bessent launches "Operation Economic Outcast" — 60+ entities across shipping, gold, aviation, tech and digital assets, secondary sanctions threatening dollar-system exclusion. Iran's rial hit a record 2.02m per dollar. Crude fell 2.4% anyway, which told you the supply story was already fully in the price.24 Aug
TALKSTuesday: Munir meets Pezeshkian in Tehran; Islamabad reports "significant progress". The mediator who brokered the June framework returned to rebuild it. Oil fell 4.9% on the day — the largest single-session drain of the war premium since the deadline lapsed.25 Aug
SHIPPINGWednesday: Iran and Oman propose a temporary joint maritime corridor plus mine clearance, permanent route targeted in 30–60 days. Plumbing rather than atmospherics — corridor administration, information exchange, maritime security services. Still no start date for the technical talks, which is the one thing left to watch.26 Aug
OILFriday: crude flat at 83.40, finishing the week down 4.05%. A quiet Friday after three weeks of daily headline repricing. The market has moved the war from event risk to background condition.28 Aug

The war de-escalated meaningfully for the first time since the memorandum lapsed, and the oil market believed it. But Friday proved the transmission has changed: the Fed chair set the rate path without reference to energy, and hike odds nearly doubled on a day crude did nothing. Watch for a start date on the Iran–Oman technical talks — that is the remaining catalyst — but the war is no longer what prices your book.
