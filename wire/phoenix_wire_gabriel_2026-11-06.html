What actually matteredone session, filtered

A strong labour market, and a Fed that has less reason to wait

1. The jobs report undid Thursday's dovish turn. On Thursday soft labour costs and strong productivity pulled the two-year down about 12bp and December hike odds toward two-thirds. On Friday 165,000 jobs and 4.1% unemployment pushed the two-year back up 5bp and the odds back toward 78% on prediction markets. The Fed meets on 8-9 December with a labour market that is not cooling.

2. The chip bounce was a relief rally, not a reversal. AMD and Nvidia rebounded and Trump backed the AI build-out, but most stocks fell and the equal-weight index was down. Thursday's question, how much data-centre building a Democratic Congress will allow, has no answer yet.

3. Earnings split hard. Airbnb rose 11.67% on a beat and a buyback of up to $12bn, and Cloudflare 9.91% on revenue up 39%. DraftKings fell 21.30% after cutting its outlook, Axon 18.18% on a lower outlook and Atlassian 16.15% on cautious cloud guidance. In a nervous market, misses are being punished much harder than beats are rewarded.

Iran was reported to be reviewing mediator proposals while seeking sanctions relief and an end to the US naval blockade. Traders continued to weigh the diplomacy against the risk in the Strait of Hormuz, where no reopening has been agreed.

Crude barely moved on the day and fell hard on the week. WTI settled $90.34, down 0.20%, and is down 13.23% from $104.12 a week ago, most of it on Monday's talk of peace and Wednesday's election result. Brent finished near $97.79.

For this book the channel is the same. Cheaper crude eases inflation, but a 165,000 jobs month means the Fed has other reasons to hike in December.
