The taperanked by how unusual
Sigma (σ) measures a move against that instrument’s own recent behaviour, not against zero. One sigma is a normal day. Two sigma is a move bigger than roughly 95% of its last twenty sessions — rare enough to be information rather than noise. A 1.8% day on the S&P is ordinary in a volatile month and remarkable in a calm one; sigma is what tells the difference.

The war · the dayWTI $101.41 · Brent touched $110

They aimed a ballistic missile at the port that exists to avoid Hormuz

A Saudi-led coalition intercepted a ballistic missile aimed at Yanbu. That is the detail to hold onto. Yanbu is a Red Sea export terminal, and its strategic value is precisely that crude leaving from there does not pass through the Strait of Hormuz — it is the hedge against the chokepoint. Targeting it is an attempt to close the alternative. The missile was intercepted and nothing was damaged, and crude still rose 3.51% to $101.41, with Brent at $109.86 after touching $110 for the first time since May.

Two supply facts arrived with it. Ukrainian strikes damaged the Ryazan refinery and shut a primary crude unit, which goes straight to the question of how much Russian refining capacity is actually running — the binding constraint the IEA named on Wednesday when it reported combined Middle East and Russian gasoil exports down more than 60% year on year to 820,000 barrels a day. And the EIA reported US crude stocks down 8.6m barrels to 415.6m for the week to 9 October, well beyond expectations, with distillates down a further 2.5m. Kpler warned that the recovery in Hormuz flows would be uneven.

And then the releases, which did not work. The IEA said over 20m barrels of additional emergency supply would be released and Washington offered up to 17m barrels through a Strategic Petroleum Reserve exchange. Announcing something close to 37m barrels of extra supply into a rising market is the strongest tool short of policy that either body has, and the price went up three and a half percent on the day and 11.7% across the week on this brief's own series. Set that beside Aramco's standing figure — commercial inventory under 6bn barrels against roughly 10bn before the crisis, less than 10% practically available — and the mechanism is clear: there is no cushion for a release to land on.

What it means here is unchanged in form and larger in size. Nothing in this book is an oil position. But gasoline did over half of September's CPI increase and rose 20.3% at the producer level, and crude has now risen from $90.76 to $101.41 in five sessions. The October print on 10 November carries that, and the one thing this week proved is that the long end will not stay up on an energy print — it fell 17 basis points through two hot releases. The risk to these multiples is no longer obviously the Fed. It is whether the AI thesis that supports three of the four holdings survives contact with its own cost of capital.
