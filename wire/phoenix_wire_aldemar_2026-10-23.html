The taperanked by how unusual
Sigma (σ) measures a move against that instrument’s own recent behaviour, not against zero. One sigma is a normal day. Two sigma is a move bigger than roughly 95% of its last twenty sessions — rare enough to be information rather than noise. A 1.8% day on the S&P is ordinary in a volatile month and remarkable in a calm one; sigma is what tells the difference.

The war · the dayBrent $116.72 · WTI $103.27 · SPR lowest since 1982

Energy at record highs, and the reserve that was supposed to cap it is empty

Start with the number that is not a price. The Department of Energy authorised an emergency exchange of up to 400,000 barrels from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve to cover a disruption at Phillips 66's Lake Charles refinery in Louisiana — and the reserve it came from is at its lowest level since 1982, after the drawdowns of this war. Four hundred thousand barrels is a rounding error against American daily consumption; the point is the direction. The instrument that exists to cap an oil spike is now being used to patch single refineries, which means it is no longer available to do the other job.

The price went where that implies. Brent settled $116.72, up $3.55 or 3.14%, its highest close since late April and 6% higher across the week; WTI settled $103.27, up $3.80 or 3.82%, 2.3% on the week. The cause was a series of Houthi attacks across Saudi Arabia, including on energy infrastructure, and Saudi Aramco ordered staff to evacuate its Dhahran headquarters on Thursday as the fighting intensified. Exxon Mobil, Chevron and ConocoPhillips all closed at all-time highs. Energy was one of the two leading groups in the index on a day the index fell.

And the supply damage is now arriving from four directions at once. October is the worst month for attacks on commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters on Washington Institute data cited by Reuters. An oil spill from an unidentified tanker off Umm Al Quwain in the UAE added to the fears. Production at Libya's Hamada and Tahara fields fell after a pipeline valve was shut — an entirely unrelated outage on the other side of the continent. And the American supply response is not coming: Baker Hughes counted 603 oil and gas rigs, unchanged for a second consecutive week, which is a drilling industry declining to respond to a hundred-dollar barrel.

The political line is the one to watch into 3 November. Trump said on Friday that Washington could end the war with Iran quickly — two days after the New York Times reported that the US and Israel have drawn up plans for renewed strikes after the midterms, and one day after he accused Iran of making up fake hits on ships to drive the oil price up before the vote. Those three statements do not fit together, and the oil market is pricing the middle one. For this book the mechanism is now explicit: crude at a six-month high pushed the ten-year back up four basis points on Friday, which is the same channel that took the thirty-year mortgage to 7.40% and broke September's housing starts.
