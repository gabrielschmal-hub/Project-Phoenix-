The taperanked by how unusual
Sigma (σ) measures a move against that instrument’s own recent behaviour, not against zero. One sigma is a normal day. Two sigma is a move bigger than roughly 95% of its last twenty sessions — rare enough to be information rather than noise. A 1.8% day on the S&P is ordinary in a volatile month and remarkable in a calm one; sigma is what tells the difference.

The AI trade split into two, and this book is on both sides of the line

1. Wednesday separated selling AI governance from paying for AI capacity. Okta launched a consortium to secure AI agents and rose 4.46%. Palantir, which sells the control layer, rose 3.68%. Alphabet raised 2026 capex to $195-205bn in the same week OpenAI cut GPT-6 API pricing by roughly half and Anthropic shipped Opus 5.5 about 40% cheaper — and fell 3.58%. The market is now paying for the toll booth and charging for the road. That is a cleaner framework than the single AI-narrative correlation this brief has tracked since 14 September, and it is more useful because the two halves can be held separately.

2. Rates broke out on strong data, which is the harder version of this problem. The ten-year set a 52-week high at 5.106% and the five-year cleared 5% for the first time since 2007 — not on inflation, not on oil, but on a PMI showing business activity at a five-year high. For the past week cheaper crude pulled yields down and let technology run. That channel is now closed and replaced by one where good news raises the discount rate. Every position in this book is a multiple; a five-and-a-bit percent five-year is the single most direct threat to all of them at once.

3. Okta gave the market a story and withheld the numbers. A consortium with AWS, Google Cloud, CrowdStrike and five others is a genuine competitive fact, and 25-37.5% pricing uplift on AI Agents deals is a genuine commercial one. But management issued no new financial targets, and the figure it did reference was 11% revenue growth on $3.07bn. The stock trades at 139 times earnings and 11.8% above the street's average target. Product leadership at that multiple has to convert into accelerating revenue, and the company chose not to say when.
