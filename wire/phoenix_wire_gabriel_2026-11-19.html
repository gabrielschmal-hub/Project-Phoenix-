The sell-off broadened, and the morning bounces failed

1. The market is in a correction-like slide. Six straight losses, 8.2% off the record, more than 90% of stocks lower and the S&P below its 200-day average in fund terms. The pattern of higher opens that fail, seen in Nvidia, Alphabet and Palantir, is typical of a market where sellers are in control.

3. Good earnings are not being rewarded. Walmart beat and raised and fell; Nvidia beat by $6.7bn on Tuesday and has fallen since. After the close Intuit fell about 9.5% as it agreed to sell Mailchimp, Ross about 5% despite a raised outlook, while Gap rose about 3.6%.

A fuel pledge from three leaders, and more mine reports in Hormuz

Trump, Xi and Putin met in Shenzhen and issued a joint declaration that their countries plan to increase the production and supply of refined petroleum products, including diesel and gasoline. No targets or dates were given, and the text read here said nothing about Iran.

Iran's navy said two oil tankers hit mines and exploded in the Strait of Hormuz, a claim that could not be independently verified. The IEA estimates the conflict has cost 2.8bn barrels of oil supply so far. WTI settled about flat at $83.21 as attacks offset the surplus outlook.
