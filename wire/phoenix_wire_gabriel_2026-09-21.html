The taperanked by how unusual
Sigma (σ) measures a move against that instrument’s own recent behaviour, not against zero. One sigma is a normal day. Two sigma is a move bigger than roughly 95% of its last twenty sessions — rare enough to be information rather than noise. A 1.8% day on the S&P is ordinary in a volatile month and remarkable in a calm one; sigma is what tells the difference.

Positioning. After the largest expiry of the quarter cleared on Friday, Monday was the first session in weeks without a scheduled event hanging over it — and it produced the biggest move. What actually matteredone session, filtered

Both sides of the AI trade rallied at once, so the concentration paid instead of hurt

1. One week after an essay asked AI companies to slow down, compute had its best day in months. On 14 September Anthropic's Dario Amodei argued for deliberately slowing frontier development and the chipmakers sold off while money moved into governance and security. On 21 September Meta's Muse agent revived the CPU-demand thesis and Intel, AMD and Arm rallied by double digits. The capex story did not die; it paused for a week. What matters for this book is that both halves of that trade rose on the same day — the compute names and the safety names — which is not a correlation that can be relied on to repeat.

2. Crude under $100 changes the arithmetic behind the dot plot. Twelve of eighteen Fed members projected another hike this year while WTI was above $102. It is now $97.79, down 6.1% from Tuesday's peak, and the supply picture has improved faster than the market expected: satellite imagery shows Saudi Arabia moving 2.8m barrels a day through Hormuz over the past six days against 700,000 in August, on top of Aramco's plan to restore half the East-West pipeline. Petrol carried August CPI to 3.4%. If this holds into the October print, the case for the projected hike weakens before the committee meets again on 28 October.

3. OKTA goes into its investor summit priced above the street. At 191.33 it trades 4.1% above the $183.71 average target, with BTIG at $219 and BofA and Wells Fargo at $200 on one side and Bernstein's downgrade to Market Perform on the other. A stock above its consensus target going into a scheduled event has to be given a reason to go higher; it does not need one to go lower. The position is +0.43R with a 15% cushion to the stop, so an event-day drop costs gain rather than capital — but the asymmetry is worth naming before the webcast, not after it.

The war · the dayWTI under $100 · Hormuz flows up fourfold

Diplomacy and a working strait took crude below $100 for the first time since the pipeline was cut

President Trump said he would probably be open to meeting Iran's President Pezeshkian at the UN General Assembly, where Pezeshkian is travelling this week — while warning of consequences if there is no deal. It is the first opening toward direct talks in the conflict, and crude sold off on it: WTI fell 2.50% to $97.79 and Brent 2.49% to $101.30.

The more durable news is physical. Satellite imagery shows Saudi Arabia moved about 2.8m barrels a day through the Strait of Hormuz over the past six days, against roughly 700,000 in August — a fourfold recovery through the very chokepoint the market had written off. Combined with Aramco's plan to bring half the East-West pipeline back within days, the supply shock that took WTI to $104 a week ago is largely being routed around. Talks can fail; barrels that are already moving are harder to take back.

The transmission is working in reverse. Crude up pushed petrol up, which pushed CPI to 3.4% and hike odds above 90%. Crude down is now pulling the ten-year back under five percent and giving long-duration technology room to run — which is most of why the Nasdaq cleared two sigma on Monday.
