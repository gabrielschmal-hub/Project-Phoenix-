The Fed blinks and the tape turns — but Palantir breaks on its own
New York Fed president John Williams told markets the yield surge reflects "a strong US economy" and investment in AI and data centres rather than inflation, said there is "no clear science right now whether monetary policy currently is sufficient", and left the impression that September is not settled. ADP then printed 38,000 private jobs against 47,000 expected, the weakest since January. Yields backed off their multi-year highs and everything rallied: S&P +0.46% to 7,666.60, Dow +0.56%, Nasdaq +0.45%, Russell +1.13%, with nine of eleven sectors higher and the VIX down to 15.20. Oil still rose — WTI 91.01, Brent 95.63 — because the war escalated on the same day: US forces struck close to 60 installations around the Strait of Hormuz and escorted 40 vessels carrying 18 million barrels through it, a wartime record. In the book BAC finally moved, +0.98% to 62.60 with its best high since 19 August. PLTR did the opposite and it was not the tape's fault.

The war · US–Irancampaign · ~60 installations struck · 18m barrels escorted in a day · strait forced open, not agreed open

America stops trying to end the war and starts running the strait

STRIKEClose to 60 Iranian military installations struck around the strait — air defence, radar, maritime assets, mine-laying and communications. Trump described the result plainly: "They can't see it because they have no radar, because we blew it up." The targeting was not punitive but functional — blinding the Iranian side of the waterway. One strike hit a wedding venue at Kuhestak in southern Iran, killing five people including a child and wounding at least 68; Iranian state media also reported hits on airports and power infrastructure in Hormozgan.CNN · NPR · 2026-09-02
ESCORTUS forces escorted 40 commercial vessels carrying 18 million barrels of oil through the strait in a single day — a wartime peak. Pre-conflict flow was roughly 20 million barrels a day, so this is close to normal throughput under naval escort. During the operation American forces shot down anti-ship cruise missiles and repelled multiple waves of drones. Officials say work against Iran's nuclear programme has been "backburnered" in favour of controlling the shipping lanes and blockading Iranian ports. That is the strategic shift, and it is the reason crude rose less than 1% on a day with sixty strikes.CNN · 2026-09-02
RETALIATIONIran fires ballistic missiles at a US base in Jordan and drones at US positions in Bahrain and Kuwait — all intercepted. Jordan's military brought down ten missiles and three landed in unpopulated ground; Kuwait confirmed intercepting the drones. The IRGC promised "crushing and devastating blows". Two rounds of Iranian retaliation have now produced no casualties and no damage, which tells you something about the current balance — and Trump has said further retaliation will be met "at a much harder and higher level".NPR · Navy Times · 2026-09-02
SHIPPINGCharter rates above $500,000 a day and war-risk insurance still elevated — the industry is not convinced. The escorts are working and the commercial market is pricing as if they might not keep working. One analyst noted the administration has "not covered itself with credibility". This gap between operational success and commercial confidence is the single most useful thing to watch: if charter rates and insurance come down, the oil premium goes with them and Friday's inflation arithmetic changes.CNN · 2026-09-02

Yesterday's question was whether the strait closes. Wednesday answered it for now: it is being forced open, convoy by convoy, and Trump says he has "almost total control" of it. That is why the tape could rally through sixty airstrikes — the market prices barrels moving, not munitions fired. Two things would break the frame. One is a successful attack on an escorted convoy, which would reprice insurance instantly. The other is Iran accepting the June memorandum it keeps offering to honour, which would take the premium out of crude just as fast in the other direction. Until one happens, the war is an oil-logistics story and the Fed is the market's actual subject.
