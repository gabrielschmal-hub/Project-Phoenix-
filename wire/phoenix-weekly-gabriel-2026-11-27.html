Most of the previous week's fall was recovered in four sessions. The Russell rose 6.53% at 2.23σ, the Dow 5.46% at 2.02σ, the S&P 5.60% at 1.93σ and the Nasdaq 6.68% at 1.90σ. Gold rose 3.08% at 0.84σ, and WTI was about flat, -0.40% at 0.07σ.

The indices, precisely. The S&P rose 387.10 points to 7,297.49, the Nasdaq 1,511.77 to 24,129.50, the Dow 2,627.61 to 50,774.94 and the Russell 168.51 to 2,750.30. Spot gold rose from $3,535.00 to $3,644.05, and the VIX fell from 35.89 to 24.01. With one session left, the S&P is down about 3.7% for November.

Four sessions. Monday: Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang and the shares rose 31.48%; the S&P rose 4.73% and the Nasdaq 6.51%. Tuesday: chips fell 7.88% and the Nasdaq 1.48%, while the Dow closed back above 50,000 and consumer confidence jumped to 103.3. Wednesday: core PCE came in at 3.3% against 3.2% expected, GDP was revised up to 4.3% and new tariffs were proposed; the Nasdaq fell as much as 4.6% before closing 0.62% lower. Thursday: closed for Thanksgiving. Friday: a half-day, with the S&P up 1.60% and the Nasdaq 2.31%.

Rates moved less than stocks. The two-year rose on Monday to about 4.67-4.70% as money left bonds, and the ten-year eased to about 4.85% by Wednesday. A December hike stayed close to fully priced all week, and futures began to price a second hike in January.

What actually matteredfour sessions, filtered

Huang's return lifted stocks; the Fed and tariffs kept the brakes on

1. Nvidia's leadership question was settled. The board brought Huang back on Monday, saying senior US officials saw no security risk, and Nvidia ended the week about 20% higher at 150.49. The reported SEC accounting inquiry and export rules are still open, and chips swung 8-14% a day early in the week.

2. The economy and inflation are both running hot. Core PCE at 3.3%, growth revised up to 4.3%, claims at 189,000 and confidence at 103.3 all support the Fed's plan to hike in December. Markets have started to price January too.

3. New risks came from trade and oil. The proposed tariffs of up to 20% would not start before January, but they add to inflation risk. Attacks on Saudi oil sites lifted Brent about 3% for the week.

The war · the weekWTI (Jan) $83.41 → $83.08 · Brent $95.44

The war shifted toward Saudi oil sites

Attacks spread to Saudi Arabia. The Houthis claimed attacks near Ras Tanura on Wednesday, and on Thursday a projectile hit Aramco's Jazan refinery, killing a worker; Saudi Arabia blamed the Houthis. Iran struck Bahrain over the previous weekend, and attacks on ships around Hormuz continued all week.

Diplomacy got more time but no deal. Trump said he may resume the war next year if talks fail, Qatar said it is working to revive negotiations, and the US blockade of Iranian ports stays in place.

WTI ended the week about flat on the January contract, after settling near $80 on Monday, while Brent rose about 3% to $95.44, its first weekly gain of November. OPEC+ meets on Sunday and is expected to keep output targets unchanged.
