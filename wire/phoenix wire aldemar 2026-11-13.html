What actually matteredone session, filtered

Chips gave back gains before Nvidia reports

1. The AI hardware trade is nervous going into Nvidia. Nvidia lost its founder as CEO on Thursday, HSBC cut it to hold on Friday, and it ended the week about 13% below its 6 November close. AMD gave back most of Thursday's rally and Cisco fell as analysts flagged weaker orders from the largest cloud companies. Tuesday's results will be read for both the numbers and the new leadership.

2. Inflation data were mixed but not soft. Headline PPI beat forecasts on the low side, but the annual rate of 6.5% is the highest in nearly four years, and core rose 0.3%. The ten-year rose to 5.15% and the dollar to its highest since April 2025. A December hike is close to certain.

3. Oil is being pulled two ways. The IEA said the market is back in surplus this quarter for the first time since the war began, while attacks on ships and Saudi facilities continued. WTI ended the week down 2.50%.

The war · the dayWTI $88.56 · Brent $95.84

Trump turns away from talks, and the IEA sees a surplus

Trump told Vice President Vance to focus on domestic issues rather than talks with Tehran, a day after posting that Washington does not need a deal with Iran. Axios reported the Mossad director was in Washington for talks, with Israel pressing to resume attacks. Verisk Maplecroft counted 36 confirmed attacks on commercial ships in the Gulf, Hormuz and the Gulf of Oman in October, the most of any month in the war.

The IEA says supply has caught up. It expects the market to be in surplus this quarter for the first time since the war began, with supply up 5.3m barrels a day since August, and member countries will release 71m barrels of emergency stocks through February. WTI settled $88.56, down 0.20%, and Brent $95.84, both down for a second week.

For this book the channel is the same. More supply caps crude and eases inflation at the margin, but the Fed is responding to inflation already in the data.
