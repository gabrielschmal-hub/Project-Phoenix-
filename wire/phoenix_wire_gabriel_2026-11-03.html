Democrats flip North Carolina as chips bounce — and AMD beats after the close
On election day the Nasdaq rose 0.83% and the S&P 0.36% as the chip index rebounded 2.63%, while the Dow fell 0.38% and most stocks fell. Job openings and factory orders beat, and a December hike is priced at about 78%. The US struck Iran's Qeshm Island and Vance spoke of a possible settlement; WTI eased to $96.09. After the close AMD beat and guided higher, and Democrats flipped North Carolina's Senate seat, with control of both chambers not yet projected. The book is flat; Q4 is +34.64 realised, heat 0R.
In five minutesthree things
1Democrats flipped North Carolina's Senate seat in the first big call of the night. Networks projected Roy Cooper the winner there, and Jon Ossoff held Georgia. When this brief was written no network had projected control of either chamber, and CNBC rated the House as leaning Democratic. Before the polls closed, prediction markets priced Democrats at about 94% to win the House and 75% the Senate. Index futures were little changed in the evening.
2Chips bounced, and AMD and Arista rose after hours. The semiconductor index rose 2.63%, recovering part of Friday's 7.20% fall, with AMD +3.65%, Broadcom +3.07% and Nvidia +2.29%. After the close AMD reported record revenue of $13.4bn and EPS of $2.09 against $1.94 expected, guided Q4 to about $17bn against about $16.2bn, and was about 6.6% higher by 7pm. Arista was up about 11% after hours. Apple fell 1.90% on its margin forecast.
3The US struck Iran's Qeshm Island, and oil still slipped. A US official called the strikes defensive, after Iran fired at US aircraft. Vance said he and Trump feel pretty good about a negotiated settlement, while an Iranian official said the two sides remain far from a deal. WTI settled $96.09, down 0.38%, a two-week low, and Brent $103.60, down 1.12%. Job openings rose to 7.52m against 7.15m expected, and December hike odds were about 78%.

An election-day pause. Nothing reached one sigma. The Nasdaq rose 0.83% at 0.68σ and the S&P 0.36% at 0.52σ, ending a three-day losing run, while the Dow fell 0.38% at 0.63σ. The Russell rose 0.23% at 0.23σ, gold fell 0.39% at 0.30σ and WTI 0.38% at 0.15σ. Under the indices Monday's rotation reversed: technology led and most stocks fell.

The indices, precisely. The S&P rose 27.52 points to 7,568.92 and the Nasdaq 207.69 to 25,372.01. The Dow fell 197.26 to 51,786.72. The Russell rose 6.57 to 2,812.45. Decliners beat gainers by about 941 to 551, and the equal-weight S&P fell 0.33%. Technology rose 1.31% and communication services 1.18%; consumer staples fell 2.07% and materials 2.01%. The VIX eased 1.51% to 20.83.

Strong data pushed yields up. JOLTS job openings rose 333,000 to 7.52m, above the 7.15m expected, and factory orders rose 1.8% against 1.3% expected, the biggest gain in five months. The ten-year closed near 4.95% and the two-year near 4.61%. A December hike is priced at about 78%, up from about 72% on Monday.

Commodities. WTI settled $96.09, down 0.38%, and Brent $103.60, down 1.12%. Spot gold fell 0.39% to $3,931.68. The dollar index was little changed at 101.87.

What actually matteredone session, filtered

The chip sell-off paused, and the election result is still coming in

1. The semiconductors got a reprieve. After Friday's 7.20% fall and more selling on Monday, the chip index rose 2.63%. After the close AMD beat on earnings and guided Q4 above expectations, and Arista beat on revenue, and both rose after hours. Wednesday will show whether that holds once election results settle.

2. The rotation reversed for a day. On Monday most stocks rose while technology fell; on Tuesday technology rose while most stocks fell, with staples and materials down about 2%. Neither day settles the question of where money goes after October's 5.3% Nasdaq decline.

3. The data keep backing a December hike. Job openings and factory orders both beat, quits rose to their highest since March 2025 and layoffs fell to their lowest since then. Hike odds rose to about 78%. A Democratic Congress would not change the Fed's December decision, but it would change the fiscal picture the Fed projects into 2027.

The war · the dayWTI $96.09 · Brent $103.60

US strikes on Qeshm Island, and talk of a deal on the same day

The US carried out what an official called defensive strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island, the largest island in the strait, after Iran fired at US aircraft. Trump told Axios that one way or the other, we will do something, but there is no rush, adding we crippled them financially. Vance said he and Trump feel pretty good about the prospects for a negotiated settlement.

The deal reports conflicted. One Arab outlet reported that Washington had approved a Qatar-presented draft memorandum with Iran, while an Iranian official said the two sides remain far from a deal. Qatar-brokered talks continued. Brent rose early on the strikes, then turned lower on the reports of progress.

Crude barely moved after Monday's 7% fall. WTI settled $96.09, down 0.38%, a two-week low, and Brent $103.60, down 1.12%. Trump had said there would be no strike before the vote; Tuesday's was described as defensive. For this book the channel is unchanged: crude feeds inflation, and inflation keeps a December hike priced at about 78%.
