Crude fell 7.8% across the week, from $100.70 to $92.89, by way of $89.42 on Tuesday and a rebound to the mid-nineties on Thursday. At 0.98 sigma it led the board, though only just — crude has been this volatile for a month, so a 7.8% week is now close to ordinary for it. Nothing on the board cleared two sigma on a weekly basis.

The equity week belonged to technology. The Nasdaq rose 2.06% to 27,068.72, the S&P 1.21% to 7,743.41, the Dow 0.28% and the Russell fell 0.38%. Monday did most of it: a 2.43 sigma Nasdaq session and a 2.27 sigma S&P session, on Meta's Muse agent reviving the CPU-demand trade — Intel +12.17%, AMD +9.95% and past a trillion dollars, Arm double digits, Meta itself +11.34%. That was one week after an essay from Anthropic's Dario Amodei asked AI companies to slow down.

The curve is the story that did not stop. The ten-year went from 4.94% to 5.225% across the week, its highest since 2007, making fresh 52-week highs on four consecutive sessions. The five-year cleared 5% for the first time since 2007 on Wednesday and held it. The trigger was not oil and not inflation: a PMI showing business activity at a five-year high. Growth data raising the discount rate is the version of this problem that cheaper energy cannot offset.

And equities rose into it anyway. The S&P was flat on Thursday and up 0.51% on Friday while the long end made new highs both days. Either earnings can carry a five-percent curve or the market has not marked it yet; there is no data to settle that until 28 October. Gold fell 1.91% across the week as the geopolitical premium came out, and bitcoin held above $84,000.

What actually matteredfive sessions, filtered

The AI trade split in two, and the rate channel switched from oil to growth

1. Selling AI governance and paying for AI capacity are now separate trades. On Wednesday Okta launched a consortium to secure AI agents and rose 4.46%; Palantir, which sells the control layer, rose 3.68%; Alphabet raised 2026 capex to $195-205bn and fell 3.58% — all in the same session. For ten days this brief tracked those three names as one AI-narrative exposure. That framing is now wrong and the better one is simpler: the market is paying for the toll booth and charging for the road. It matters because the two halves can be held separately, and because they will re-couple on any day the whole theme is repriced.

2. The rate channel changed underneath all of it. For a week, falling crude pulled the curve down and let long-duration technology run. Then Wednesday's PMI showed business activity at a five-year high, the five-year Treasury cleared 5% for the first time since 2007, and the ten-year made fresh highs on four consecutive sessions to 5.225%. Good growth is now the rate risk, and cheaper oil cannot offset it. Every position in this book is a long-duration multiple, so this is the single most consequential change of the week — and the equity market's response so far has been to rise.

3. Valuation reasserted itself at the end of the week, in the one place this book is most exposed. Bernstein downgraded Palo Alto, Okta and SentinelOne on Friday because the group had run roughly 100% this year, and Zscaler — named in Okta's own alliance two days earlier — fell 8.64%. Morgan Stanley took the other side, arguing AI-driven security spending justifies the multiples. Both are arguments about price rather than demand, which is the specific risk of owning a re-rated sector: the business can keep improving while the multiple compresses.

The war · the weekcrude −7.8% · Hormuz offered, Yanbu struck

Tehran offered to reopen the strait and then missiles were fired at the terminal

Tuesday was the offer. A senior Iranian official said Tehran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days if Washington eased military pressure and lifted its blockade of Iranian ports — a proposal delivered through mediators six days earlier. Secretary of State Rubio said the administration was open to that. WTI fell 8.6% to $89.42, a 2.68 sigma session and the largest single reading on this board in a month. A Trump-Pezeshkian meeting was ruled out.

Thursday was the escalation. Iran-aligned Houthi forces fired missiles at Saudi cities including Yanbu and Taif. Yanbu is the Red Sea terminal the East-West pipeline runs to — the endpoint of the entire workaround Aramco has been building since 13 September. Talks stalled at the UN, with Pezeshkian telling the General Assembly Iran would not bend at the knee and an official conceding the sides remained far apart. Crude rebounded 3.9%. A pipeline that can be repaired is a six-week problem; a terminal that can be hit is not.

Friday was the offer again, and crude eased 1.82% to $92.89 on renewed truce discussion under Qatari mediation. Across the week crude fell 7.8%, and it is now $11 below where it traded when twelve of eighteen Fed members projected another hike. Monday's supporting evidence still stands: satellite imagery showed Saudi Arabia moving 2.8m barrels a day through Hormuz against 700,000 in August. But the price now moves on statements rather than barrels, and a negotiation is not a trend.
