The taperanked by how unusual
Sigma (σ) measures a move against that instrument’s own recent behaviour, not against zero. One sigma is a normal day. Two sigma is a move bigger than roughly 95% of its last twenty sessions — rare enough to be information rather than noise. A 1.8% day on the S&P is ordinary in a volatile month and remarkable in a calm one; sigma is what tells the difference.

What actually matteredone session, filtered

The first rate rise in three years is fully priced, which is the whole problem

1. Today's hike is 92.7% priced and it is not the event. Twenty-five basis points to 3.75-4.00% would be the Fed's first increase since July 2023, and the market has had weeks to absorb it. What is not settled is the dot plot: the June projections showed one quarter-point rise by end-2026, and the bond market now prices one more this year and one to two in 2027. If the median dot moves up to meet the curve, the repricing is in 2027, not today. Chair Warsh has made price stability his stated priority and prefers minimalist communication — which removes the forward guidance that would normally cushion a hawkish projection.

2. PLTR and OKTA both still move on one story, and that story got more institutional this week. OpenAI confirmed it is working with Anthropic and Google DeepMind on AI safety. What began as an essay is becoming coordination between the three labs, which makes the trade behind these positions more durable than a one-day sentiment move — and also more crowded. The concentration is in a narrative, not a sector, so nothing in a screener would flag it. If it unwinds, two thirds of this book moves together.

3. Gold fell through a widening oil shock, and that is the single most informative price on the board. The Saudi pipeline is still shut, Brent is at $107.90, and bullion dropped 0.73% to $4,320.10 with silver down 0.76%. A geopolitical supply disruption that pushes gold down is a market telling you that real yields outrank geopolitics at a five-percent ten-year. Every asset is being priced off the curve right now, which is why a dot plot matters more than a pipeline.

The war · the dayBrent $107.90 · second straight advance

Crude is no longer a headline premium, it is an input to today's decision

WTI rose 2.64% to $104.10 and Brent 2.14% to $107.90, a second consecutive advance with the Saudi East-West pipeline still shut since 13 September. The 1,200km line normally moves 4-5m barrels a day to Yanbu on the Red Sea and exists specifically to bypass the Strait of Hormuz, where flows are already down to roughly 6-9m bpd from over a fifth of global supply. Repairs are estimated at five to six weeks, so this is priced as a quarter-long problem rather than a scare.

The transmission is no longer theoretical. Petrol added 3.9% in August and carried the CPI headline to 3.4%, which moved hike odds from roughly 70% to above 90%. Today the Fed responds to a supply shock it cannot reach with a policy rate — and with crude still climbing, the same mechanism is loading the October print while the committee is still writing this one.
