Nvidia opened higher after its results, then fell 2.89%. The Fed minutes showed almost all officials expect more hikes, and Waller said a half-point is probably needed in December. The S&P fell 0.23% for a fifth straight day and the Dow 1.11%, while the Nasdaq rose 0.42%. Gold and oil each fell more than 3%. The book is flat; Q4 is +45.38 realised, heat 0R.

Sigma (σ) measures a move against that instrument’s own recent behaviour, not against zero. One sigma is a normal day. Two sigma is a move bigger than roughly 95% of its last twenty sessions — rare enough to be information rather than noise.

What actually matteredone session, filtered

A strong quarter from Nvidia met a hawkish Fed

1. Nvidia's results were not enough. Revenue beat by about $6.7bn and the guide by about $5bn, yet the shares turned from a 2.4% gain at the open to a 2.89% loss. The leadership change, an SEC inquiry reported last week and new export limits weigh more than the numbers for now.

2. The Fed is leaning toward a bigger December move. The minutes showed broad support for more hikes, a couple of officials open to more than a quarter-point, and Waller now says a half-point is probably needed. The two-year held at 4.80%; markets still price a quarter-point as most likely.

3. Inflation hedges fell, not rose. Gold and oil both fell more than 3% as the dollar rose and US crude stocks built. Falling oil helps the inflation outlook, but the Fed is reacting to inflation already in the data.

Iran targets bases in Kuwait and Bahrain, and oil falls anyway

Iran targeted a US air base in Kuwait and sites in Bahrain after a US strike on Qeshm Island; both countries said they intercepted the attacks. Trump is due to meet Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen on Thursday, with the Iran war expected on the agenda.

Crude fell on supply. WTI settled $83.26, down 3.20%, its lowest close since late August, after US crude stocks rose 3.38m barrels; Brent settled $91.95, down 1.28%. The December WTI contract expires on Friday.

For this book the channel is the same. Cheaper crude eases inflation at the margin, but the Fed's minutes show it is focused on hiking anyway.
