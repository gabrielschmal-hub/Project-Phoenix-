A week that looked quiet on the cards and was not quiet at all. Gold fell 2.96% at 1.64σ, after falling 4.14% the week before. Behind it the Nasdaq fell 2.00% at 0.99σ, the Dow 0.85% at 0.89σ, the Russell rose 1.29% at 0.83σ, the S&P fell 0.57% at 0.57σ and WTI rose 0.82% at 0.14σ. Nothing reached two sigma on the week, because the gains of Monday and Tuesday and the losses of Thursday and Friday nearly cancelled.

The indices, precisely. The S&P fell 43.61 points, or 0.6%, to 7,580.07, a third straight weekly loss; the Nasdaq 518.34, or 2.0%, to 25,437.00; the Dow 444.85, or 0.9%, to 51,770.31. Only the Russell rose, 35.94 points or 1.3%, to 2,811.84. For October the Nasdaq fell about 5.3%, its worst month since March 2025, while the Dow rose.

Five sessions, two halves. Monday: the S&P +0.70% and the Nasdaq +0.82% after Meta launched a plan to raise up to $95bn of equity for AI infrastructure. Tuesday: the Nasdaq +1.18% and the Russell +1.16% as banks and Blackstone arranged $63bn for Anthropic to buy Broadcom chips. Wednesday: a calm session into the Fed decision, with crude +2.88%. Thursday: the Nasdaq −1.38% as Alphabet fell 12.29%, with the Russell +1.01%. Friday: the S&P −1.82% at 2.53σ and the Nasdaq −2.86% at 2.30σ, the biggest daily drops since at least mid-September, as Intel fell 28.58% and the chip index 7.20%. The market spent the first half paying for AI funding and the second half marking down AI cash flow.

The bond market rallied through all of it. The ten-year started the week near 5.0%, rose to 5.044% after the Fed on Wednesday, and finished at about 4.94% on Treasury's curve. The two-year ended at 4.56%. December hike odds went from about 83% on Tuesday to above 80% after the Fed and PCE, then eased to about 76% on Friday as stocks fell.

Gold is the reading to explain. In a week with a mine attack on tankers, Hormuz declared closed again and crude at its highest settle in weeks, gold fell 3.62% on Friday alone to $3,875.84 spot, with December futures at a one-year low. It is trading on the Fed, not the Gulf: two officials argued for more hikes on Friday, and a metal with no yield loses most when rates are expected to rise.

What actually matteredfive sessions, filtered

The market paid for AI funding early in the week and punished AI cash flow late

1. Three hyperscalers reported, and the verdicts followed free cash flow. Microsoft, whose operating cash covered its capex by $18.6bn, beat and ended Thursday nearly flat. Alphabet missed on revenue and EPS for the first time in nearly four years, with free cash flow of minus $9.07bn beside Cloud growth of 103%, and fell 12.29%, its worst day in more than two decades. Amazon grew AWS 39% and set a record margin, but trailing free cash flow was minus $42.4bn; it fell 1.41% on Friday after opening 4.6% lower.

2. On Friday the doubt reached the companies that sell to them. Intel's Q4 guide of $0.25 a share against about $0.43 took it down 28.58%, and the semiconductor index fell 7.20%, with AMD and Qualcomm down more than 10%. Monday's and Tuesday's rallies were about financing: Meta's $95bn equity plan and $63bn for Anthropic's chip purchases. By Friday the question was whether the spending earns its keep, and the answer the market gave was no, not yet.

3. The Fed leaned toward hiking, and the data gave it no reason to stop. The hold at 3.75-4.00% on Wednesday came with two dissents for a hike, from Cook and Hammack. Thursday's September PCE matched forecasts at 3.6% headline and 3.1% core, Q3 GDP grew 3.4%, and Friday's employment cost index rose 3.4% on the year. Then Musalem and Logan argued for more increases, Logan for at least two. Falling stocks moved the December odds only from above 80% to about 76%.

The war · the weekWTI $103.27 → $104.12 · Brent (Jan) $113.43

Mines in the strait, Hormuz declared closed again, and talks that never quite start

Crude fell early in the week on diplomacy and rose late on attacks. On Monday and Tuesday WTI fell 3.3% to $99.90 as Qatari mediators and then Saudi, Turkish and Pakistani officials went to Tehran. On Wednesday it rose 2.88% after the Houthis claimed a strike on Saudi Arabia's largest refinery, which Saudi authorities did not confirm. It eased on Thursday and rose 2.08% to $104.12 on Friday, its highest settle since at least mid-September.

The attacks changed form. On Thursday Iran's navy said a tanker hit a naval mine east of the Strait and caught fire, and an IRGC general said the strait will remain closed until Iran's conditions are met. UK Maritime Trade Operations later said a tanker hit that night had explosives attached to its hull from a small boat, which reports called the first successful attack of that kind in the war. Iran said three tankers hit mines; Oman rescued 25 crew.

The politics are pointed at Tuesday. Trump said Iran had wanted to make a deal but kept dragging it out, said on Friday the war will be wrapped up soon, and has said Washington will not strike before the 3 November midterms. Qatari mediators returned to Tehran on Friday, and an Iranian official said Tehran welcomes efforts toward negotiations. Nothing has been signed and nothing has been scheduled. For this book the channel is the same as all month: crude feeds inflation, and inflation keeps the Fed talking about hikes.
