The taperanked by how unusual
Sigma (σ) measures a move against that instrument’s own recent behaviour, not against zero. One sigma is a normal day. Two sigma is a move bigger than roughly 95% of its last twenty sessions — rare enough to be information rather than noise. A 1.8% day on the S&P is ordinary in a volatile month and remarkable in a calm one; sigma is what tells the difference.

The war · the daydistillates +1.68m · WTI $97.15

The diesel squeeze eased from two directions at once

Tuesday's brief argued the damage had moved from crude to refined product. Wednesday pushed back on that from two sides in one session. First the inventories: distillate stocks rose 1.68m barrels to 104.3m against an expected 1.16m fall — a surprise of 2.8m barrels on the one product that has actually been squeezed, with refinery utilisation at 93.4% and crude runs up 34,000 barrels a day. Then the geopolitics: Russia agreed to resume US-mediated peace talks with Ukraine, possibly as early as November. BOK Financial's Dennis Kissler said a deal could unlock the tight global diesel supplies. That is the first credible route to easing the product shortage that does not require the Gulf to calm down.

The crude side went the other way and the price still fell. Crude stocks fell 3.06m barrels to 412.5m against an expected 820,000-barrel draw, and gasoline fell 2.95m to 205.3m against an expected 443,000 — both bullish, both larger than forecast. Cushing rose 987,000 and net crude imports rose 837,000 barrels a day. Against a draw more than three times the forecast, WTI settled $97.15, down 67 cents, having touched $100 intraday, and Brent settled $108.82, up three cents after giving up more than two dollars of gains. A market that ignores a bullish inventory print is a market pricing something else, and what it is pricing is the possibility of peace in Ukraine.

Iran hardened, and that is the other half of the session. President Masoud Pezeshkian said Washington cannot expect dialogue while blockading and threatening Iran; foreign minister Abbas Araqchi said Iran is fully prepared to defend itself. Tuesday's three-dollar reversal had rested entirely on a mediation meeting with Pakistan's army chief; twenty-four hours later the Iranian position is that there is nothing to mediate. So the diplomatic premium that took crude down on Tuesday was withdrawn on Wednesday, and crude fell anyway — which tells you the Ukraine headline is doing more work than the Iran one.

And the attacks widened geographically. Kpler counts at least 18 attacks on vessels over the past fortnight, and Kissler pointed to at least two more ships struck overnight in the Strait of Hormuz. LSEG analysts say Iran is now targeting tankers beyond the Strait, into the Gulf of Oman — which matters because the Gulf of Oman is the open-water side of the chokepoint, the stretch that re-routing was supposed to make safe. A campaign that reaches past the narrows is a campaign against the detour, and it is the reason crude flows can sit at four-fifths of pre-war levels while insurance and freight keep climbing.
