The taperanked by how unusual
Sigma (σ) measures a move against that instrument’s own recent behaviour, not against zero. One sigma is a normal day. Two sigma is a move bigger than roughly 95% of its last twenty sessions — rare enough to be information rather than noise. A 1.8% day on the S&P is ordinary in a volatile month and remarkable in a calm one; sigma is what tells the difference.

That combination — small caps and the Nasdaq up, the median stock down, the Dow pinned by one name — is a market leaning on a few large positions the day before the decision that could move all of them.

The indices, precisely. The S&P rose 45.83 points to 7,722.60, the Nasdaq 308.43 to 26,477.81, the Russell 32.45 to 2,818.87 and the Dow 15.99 to 52,377.57. Three financings in two sessions, and the bond market waiting

1. Broadcom's $63bn is the third AI financing in two sessions, and the rally is resting on them. Wall Street banks and Blackstone arranged $63bn so that Anthropic can buy Broadcom's AI chips; Broadcom rose about 3.8% and DA Davidson upgraded it to Buy with a $400 target. On Monday it was Meta's plan to raise up to $95bn of equity and SpaceX's $40bn of GPU debt. The demand for compute is not what is being tested this week; the willingness of lenders and shareholders to pay for it is, and for two sessions that willingness has been unlimited. The smaller data-centre operators that Musk warned about on Monday rebounded on Tuesday. That is exactly the condition under which a financing market looks safest, and it is worth remembering that it closes faster than demand fades.

2. The rally was narrow, and the economy underneath it was not weak. The Nasdaq rose 1.18% and the S&P 0.60%, but most S&P members fell; the Dow was held to 15.99 points by Boeing's $0.34 core loss. Against that, consumer confidence came in at 91.7 against 80.4 expected, UPS and Coca-Cola beat and raised, and Centene and HCA raised. A week ago this brief was reading September housing starts as the first clean evidence of rate damage. Tuesday's data says the consumer has not noticed yet, which is the inflation-side argument the Fed will hear today.

3. Alphabet ended its losing run on the last session before it reports. It rose 0.84% to 316.83 after five consecutive lower closes, on heavy options volume — 139,800 Class C contracts traded against 1.67m open. The question tonight is the one a GuruFocus preview frames bluntly: company-wide operating cash flow of $39.07bn against $44.92bn of capital purchases, free cash flow of minus $5.86bn, and Pichai on record that cloud revenue is being held back to feed DeepMind. Meta answered the cash question with $95bn of equity and was rewarded. Alphabet has to answer it with revenue, because it has deferred some on purpose.

The war · the dayWTI $99.90 · Brent $113.88

Crude back below $100 on talks that have not produced anything

The oil price is now pricing the diplomacy, not the damage. WTI settled $99.90, down 1.83%, its first close below $100 since 22 October, and Brent $113.88, down 1.67%, a second consecutive fall from Friday's six-month-high close of $116.72. The stated reason was attention on Middle East peace talks. The talks themselves have produced nothing on the record: officials from Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan went to Tehran on Monday and Qatari mediators on Sunday in coordination with Washington, and Iran told Pakistan's army chief there would be no talks with Washington until its demands are met.

The physical risks from the weekend have not gone away. The Houthis claimed strikes on pumping stations on Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline — the route that moves Saudi crude to the Red Sea without passing Hormuz — and on Riyadh airport; Saudi Arabia has confirmed no damage and reported further interceptions. Three of that pipeline's pumping stations were hit in September and it restarted on 22 September. The US Strategic Petroleum Reserve is at its lowest level since 1982, and the New York Times reported last week that the US and Israel have drawn up plans for renewed strikes on Iran after the 3 November midterms.

For this book the energy line runs through tonight and Thursday. A crude price back below $100 makes the Fed's statement easier to write and Thursday's PCE easier to read. Energy stocks fell more than 2% and refiners fell hardest, which is the market taking some of last week's war premium back out of the equity side as well. Gold rose 1.08% to $4,060.20 — back above four thousand for a second session — and none of the dated risk on this page has changed in the last 48 hours.
