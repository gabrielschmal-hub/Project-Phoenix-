What actually matteredone session, filtered

The AI sell-off spread from the spenders to the suppliers

1. This week the market questioned the AI spending, and on Friday it reached the chipmakers. Alphabet fell on negative free cash flow on Thursday. On Friday Intel lost 28.58% on its guide and the whole semiconductor index fell 7.20%, with AMD, Qualcomm, Broadcom and Micron all down 5% or more. Amazon, which spends more than anyone, recovered most of its early loss. The worry moved from who pays for capacity to who sells it.

2. Rates did not cause this, and the Fed is not coming to help. Yields fell and the dollar slipped, so this was not a bond scare. And two Fed presidents said on the same day that more hikes are needed, Logan saying at least two. With wages rising 3.4% a year and core PCE at 3.1%, a falling stock market does not change the December case much; hike odds eased only to about 76%.

3. Oil and gold went opposite ways. Crude rose 2.08% on a new kind of attack on tankers, while gold fell 3.62% to its lowest in about a year. Gold is trading as a rate asset, not a war hedge: higher rates for longer are worse for it than more risk in the Gulf is good for it.

The war · the dayWTI $104.12 · Brent (Jan) $113.43

A limpet-mine attack on a tanker, and Trump says the war will end soon

A tanker was hit on Thursday night by explosives attached to its hull from a small boat, UK Maritime Trade Operations said, correcting early reports of a projectile; it did not say who did it. Reports called it the first successful attack of that kind since the war began. Iran's IRGC said tankers had hit mines while using an unauthorised route, and Iran said three caught fire. Oman rescued 25 crew.

The diplomacy moved slightly. Qatari mediators returned to Tehran on Friday, and an Iranian official said Tehran welcomes any efforts aimed at negotiations. Trump said the war, now in its ninth month, will be wrapped up soon, and has said Washington will not strike before the 3 November midterms. The US also repeated that hostilities could resume.

Crude priced the attack, not the talks. WTI settled $104.12, up 2.08%, and Brent's January contract $113.43, up 2.37%. For this book the channel is unchanged: crude feeds inflation, inflation feeds a Fed that is still talking about hikes, and that pressure lands hardest on the most expensive stocks.
