Chips drag the Nasdaq down 2.5% — a day before Nvidia reports under an interim CEO
The S&P fell 1.92% and the Nasdaq 2.49% as chip stocks sold off worldwide ahead of Nvidia's results and after new US limits on Chinese access to advanced chips. Nvidia fell 4.12%, the VIX jumped to 22.91, and WTI fell 3.21% to $85.72. The book is flat; Q4 is +45.38 realised, heat 0R.
In five minutesthree things
1Chips led a broad sell-off on the eve of Nvidia's results. The S&P fell 1.92%, the Nasdaq 2.49%, the Dow 1.41% and the Russell 1.70%, a third straight loss for the S&P and Nasdaq. The semiconductor fund SMH fell 5.11%; Micron and Intel fell about 7% and AMD 5.58%. Taiwan's stock index had fallen 6.03% overnight. Only staples and utilities rose, and the VIX jumped 18.70% to 22.91.
2Nvidia fell 4.12% to 156.14 ahead of Tuesday's results. A source told CNBC the board is not discussing bringing back Jensen Huang, while an AP report said Trump backed a campaign to reinstate him. Treasury Secretary Bessent had urged a dramatic shakeup and an outside CEO. Mizuho and UBS cut their price targets. The Commerce Department also tightened rules on Chinese companies renting advanced US chips in data centres abroad.
3Oil fell 3.21% and the data softened. WTI settled $85.72, its lowest since early September, on signs of a supply surplus. The Empire State manufacturing index fell to 7.8 against 16.7 expected. Yields eased slightly: the two-year to about 4.83% and the ten-year to 5.12%. A December hike remains about 91% priced on CME's FedWatch, with 9% on a half-point.

The book is still flat. Palantir fell 4.52% to 160.39, well below the 194.25 exit, and AMZN fell 3.34% to 257.75. Q4 stays at +45.38, +2.77R, all realised, and the day was +0.00. The full 3.0R is available.

Sigma (σ) measures a move against that instrument’s own recent behaviour, not against zero. One sigma is a normal day. Two sigma is a move bigger than roughly 95% of its last twenty sessions — rare enough to be information rather than noise.

What actually matteredone session, filtered

The AI trade sold off before its biggest test

1. Nvidia's results are now about more than numbers. The company reports on Tuesday with an interim CEO, a board said to be against bringing Huang back, the Treasury Secretary calling for an outside CEO and the White House reported to be backing his return. The shares have fallen about 10% since Huang's resignation was announced.

2. Washington tightened chip access to China again. The new Commerce rule targets Chinese firms renting US chips in data centres abroad. Chip stocks fell worldwide: Taiwan's index had its second-largest point drop on record overnight, and SK hynix and TSMC fell 6-8%.

3. Some cooling at the edges. The Empire State index fell well below forecasts and oil dropped 3%, yet a December hike stays about 91% priced. Tuesday's retail sales are the next check on consumer demand.

The war · the dayWTI $85.72 · Brent $92.44

A third carrier arrives, and oil still falls on the surplus

A third US aircraft carrier has arrived in the Middle East, the AP reported, and Pakistan said it would continue with US-Iran diplomacy. Iran reported at least four tankers hit by mines in and around the Strait of Hormuz within 24 hours; that report could not be confirmed elsewhere.

Crude looked past it. WTI settled $85.72, down 3.21%, and Brent $92.44, down 3.55%, as traders focused on signs of a supply surplus after the IEA's report last week. The December WTI contract expires on Friday.

For this book the channel is the same. Cheaper crude eases inflation, but the Fed's December hike is driven by inflation already in the data.
