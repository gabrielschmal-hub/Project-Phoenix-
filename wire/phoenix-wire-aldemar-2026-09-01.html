The war · US–Irankinetic again · Larak Island strike · missiles at a US base in Jordan

Zero tolerance meets a promise of punishment

STRIKEUS forces destroy two Iranian launchers on Larak Island preparing to deploy sea mines into the strait — the first strike on Iran since late July. CENTCOM framed it as a "limited, precise action" against IRGC minelaying forces posing an "imminent threat", carried out to protect "civilian mariners, commercial shipping, and the free flow of global commerce". It follows the US completing mine clearance of the strait's main shipping lane and Trump declaring a "Zero Tolerance policy on mine placement".Axios · NBC News · CENTCOM · 2026-08-30
MILITARYIran fires ballistic missiles at a US base in Jordan; eight intercepted, no damage or casualties. The IRGC said the strike "will be answered by the sons of Iran and will result in the punishment of the aggressor", then answered proportionally and ineffectively. A response calibrated to be intercepted is a response designed not to end the diplomacy — but it is still the first exchange of fire in five weeks.Axios · 2026-08-31
OILBrent back above $88, WTI +2.87% to 85.93 — the biggest single-day gain since the deadline lapsed. Crude reversed most of last week's 4% decline in one session. The move is rational: the corridor framework assumed a strait that gets safer, and Sunday demonstrated it is being made safe by force rather than by agreement.Alain Guillot · Phoenix engine · 2026-08-31

The transmission is oil → inflation → the regime call, and it just reconnected. Ten days ago crude at $93 was holding the inflation gate open; last week's de-escalation took it to $80 and the market started pricing relief; Sunday put it back to $86 and hike odds to 62%. Note the important nuance, though — the US is striking to keep the strait open, not to close it. If the mine clearance holds and the corridor still opens, this is a violent step toward the constructive outcome rather than away from it.
