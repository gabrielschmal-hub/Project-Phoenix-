What actually matteredone session, filtered

Three hyperscalers in two days, and the market paid only for cash

1. The verdicts lined up with free cash flow. Microsoft, whose operating cash covered its capex by $18.6bn, closed Thursday nearly flat. Alphabet, with free cash flow of minus $9.07bn, fell 12.29%. Amazon, at minus $42.4bn over twelve months with capex still rising into 2027, fell about 7% after hours despite AWS speeding up to 39% and a record margin. Growth was strong at all three. The market drew its line on who pays for the spending out of cash.

2. The money stayed in the market. The Russell rose 1.01% on a day the Nasdaq fell 1.38%, and health care, real estate, staples and utilities all rose more than 1.5%. Bonds rallied too. That is a rotation away from large technology names rather than a move out of stocks, and it happened with PCE in line and a December hike priced above 80%.

3. Inflation is moving the wrong way, slowly. Core PCE rose to 3.1% and headline to 3.6%, both as expected. Real spending was flat and real disposable income fell 0.3%, while the saving rate dropped to 3.8%: households are holding consumption up by saving less. GDP at 3.4% means the Fed has no growth reason to wait, and two of its members voted to hike on Wednesday.

The war · the dayWTI $102.00 · Brent $114.88

A mine strike off Hormuz, and crude still slipped

Iran's navy said a tanker hit a naval mine east of the Strait of Hormuz and caught fire, unable to move, in what it called a restricted and unsafe area. Iran said the strait is closed to ships that pass without coordination, and an IRGC general said it will remain closed until Iran's conditions are met. The IRGC also challenged US claims to have cleared mines from the strait, asking for independent evidence.

The oil market barely reacted. WTI settled $102.00, down 0.76%, and Brent $114.88, down 0.38%. A single damaged tanker in a zone Iran already treats as closed did not change the supply picture, after Wednesday's 2.9% gain on a claimed strike on a Saudi refinery.

The diplomacy has not moved. On Tuesday Trump said Iran had wanted to make a deal but liked dragging it out, and suggested Tehran might be waiting for the election. The midterms are on 3 November, the date the reported US and Israeli strike plans have been tied to. For this book the channel is unchanged: crude feeds inflation, and inflation feeds a Fed now priced to hike in December.
