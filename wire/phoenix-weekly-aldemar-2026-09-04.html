Good morning Aldemar · week of 31 August – 4 September 2026
A week that changed everything and moved nothing — the S&P finished nine basis points higher
Between Monday and Friday the US turned a single retaliatory strike into a naval campaign, escorted 18 million barrels through the Strait of Hormuz in a day, struck Iranian oil tankers, and had ballistic missiles fired at an aircraft carrier. Three Fed speakers moved September from a near-certain hike to a coin flip and payrolls then printed at three times consensus. WTI added 9.69% to 91.48 and the 10-year rose to 4.78%. The S&P 500 closed the week at 7,718.60, up 0.09%. Everything that happened, happened underneath: PLTR fell 6.42% through a 22-point range on a competitive threat from Alphabet, GOOG fell 2.14% while Berkshire's $37.8bn stake purchase came to light, BAC gained 1.10% on the wrong days, and AAPL round-tripped a CEO handover into a bad product leak five days before its event.

The war · the weekcampaign · warships targeted · tankers struck · WTI +9.69% on the week

From one strike to a naval campaign in five sessions

strikeUS strikes escalate from two launchers at Larak to ~60 installations around the strait, then to Iranian oil tankers. Monday's action followed two Saudi supertankers being hit; Tuesday brought strikes at Qeshm Island, Bandar Abbas, Chabahar, Jask and Sirik; by the weekend the US had disabled two Iranian crude carriers and destroyed a third near Kharg Island, which CENTCOM said financed the IRGC and its proxies.2026-08-31 to 09-05
militaryThe IRGC fired ballistic missiles at a US aircraft carrier and a guided-missile destroyer. Both evaded, no personnel hurt. Iran's earlier retaliation — missiles at Jordan, drones at Bahrain and Kuwait — was intercepted in full twice. Iran warned of "a significant response" and Trump said the US may target Pickaxe Mountain "very soon", returning to the nuclear file after a week of shipping lanes.2026-09-05
oilWTI +9.69% on the week to 91.48 — and it fell on the two days the war escalated most. The US escorted 40 vessels carrying 18 million barrels through the strait in a single day, close to pre-conflict throughput. Charter rates above $500,000 a day and record diesel prices say the commercial market is less convinced than the futures curve.2026-09-04
talksTrump closes the off-ramp: "an agreement with them isn't worth the paper it's written on". Pezeshkian had spent the week offering to honour the June memorandum, which includes free navigation, if the US reciprocated. That is the fastest path to unwinding the crude premium and it is now rhetorically shut.2026-09-03

The week's lesson is that the transmission broke. Crude rose 9.69% and the S&P finished flat; missiles hit at a carrier and crude fell. Waller made September about CPI rather than oil, so a barrel no longer converts into a rate hike. That holds until an escorted convoy is successfully attacked — the one event that would reprice insurance, freight and the inflation path at once.
