A rebound week, nearly all of it on Monday. The Dow rose 2.07% at 1.90σ and the S&P 2.51% at 1.88σ, its first weekly gain after four straight losses. Gold fell 4.63% at 1.50σ, the Nasdaq rose 2.27% at 1.17σ, the Russell 1.32% at 0.89σ, and WTI fell 2.50% at 0.45σ. Nothing reached two sigma on the week.

The indices, precisely. The S&P rose 184.81 points to 7,561.67, the Nasdaq 556.85 to 25,136.27, the Dow 1,061.82 to 52,347.55 and the Russell 36.38 to 2,790.49. WTI fell from $90.83 to $88.56 and spot gold from $4,070.41 to $3,881.75.

Five sessions. Monday: the S&P +2.35% after Trump said further attacks on Iran were unlikely while talks progressed, WTI −4.38%, AMD +13% on an upgrade and Nvidia −4% on its board investigation. Tuesday: October CPI rose 0.4% with core 0.3%, both above forecast, and the two-year jumped about 17bp. Wednesday: with bonds shut for Veterans Day, chips rose about 5.7% on CoreWeave's raised guidance, then Nvidia fell about 8% after hours on a report that its board would remove Jensen Huang. Thursday: Huang resigned and Colette Kress became interim CEO; the ten-year went back above 5%, the Russell fell 1.66% and gold 2.79%. Friday: PPI rose 0.3%, a tenth below forecast, and chips fell 3.43%.

Rates rose all week. The two-year rose from about 4.56% on Monday to about 4.88%, and the ten-year about 29bp to 5.15%, its highest close in at least three weeks. A December hike went from about 84% priced on Monday to about 95% on Friday, with talk of a half-point after Waller left the door open.

What actually matteredfive sessions, filtered

Nvidia lost its founder as CEO, and inflation kept the Fed on course to hike

1. The biggest AI company changed leadership in four days. A board investigation opened on 8 November, the Wall Street Journal reported on Wednesday that the board was preparing to remove Jensen Huang, and he resigned on Thursday. Colette Kress is interim CEO. The shares ended the week about 13% below their 6 November close, and the results on 17 November will be the first without him.

2. Inflation came in hot enough to settle December. October CPI rose 0.4% with core 0.3%, both above forecast, and PPI's annual rate reached 6.5%, the highest since November 2022. Waller opened the door to a half-point, Logan and Hammack wanted more hikes, and Goldman added March to its forecast. The ten-year ended the week at 5.15%.

3. The AI trade swung stock by stock. AMD rose 13% on Monday, CoreWeave 25% on Wednesday, and chips gained 10.6% from Monday to Thursday before falling 3.43% on Friday. Apple touched a record. Last week's question about data-centre limits under a Democratic Congress has turned into a question about individual companies.

The war · the weekWTI $90.83 → $88.56

From restraint to no deal, and a supply surplus

The week opened on restraint. On Sunday Trump said further attacks on Iran were highly unlikely while talks progressed, and WTI fell 4.38% on Monday to its lowest settle since early September; Brent briefly fell below $90 on Tuesday. Trump said the war could end fairly soon.

It closed on no deal. Iran denied holding talks and said Hormuz stays shut until its demands are met. The Houthis claimed attacks on a Saudi pipeline station, a Yanbu facility and an Aramco building on Thursday, Iran's military said four tankers were hit in Hormuz, and Trump said Washington does not need a deal. On Friday he told Vance to focus on domestic issues rather than talks.

Crude still fell 2.50% on the week. The IEA said the market is back in surplus this quarter for the first time since the war began, and member countries will release 71m barrels of emergency stocks. WTI ended at $88.56.
