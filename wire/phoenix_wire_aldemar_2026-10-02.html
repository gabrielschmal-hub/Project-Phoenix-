The taperanked by how unusual
Sigma (σ) measures a move against that instrument’s own recent behaviour, not against zero. One sigma is a normal day. Two sigma is a move bigger than roughly 95% of its last twenty sessions — rare enough to be information rather than noise. A 1.8% day on the S&P is ordinary in a volatile month and remarkable in a calm one; sigma is what tells the difference.

The war · the weekG7 opens the reserves · crude $91.11

The reserves were opened because the negotiation went quiet

The G7 agreed on Friday to release up to 100 million barrels of crude and diesel over four months. It is coordinated through the International Energy Agency, works out at about 830,000 barrels a day, and prioritises diesel, with a substantial volume promised within the first 20 days. France holds the G7 presidency and Emmanuel Macron led it, after an overnight call with Trump about the possibility of US restrictions on diesel exports. The package also includes commitments to raise refinery output and not to restrict energy trade among members. Fuel prices were near record highs going in, and winter heating demand is the deadline.

The diplomatic track has produced nothing since Wednesday. Washington's reply to Iran's seven-point proposal reached Tehran through Qatari mediation on 30 September; Foreign Minister Araghchi briefed the cabinet, the contents were not disclosed, and there has been no statement from either side since. Iran had threatened new strikes while it waited, and the Revolutionary Guard disputed on Tuesday that the strait had been restored at all. Nothing has been added to that picture in three days.

So the week's oil story is that policy overtook diplomacy. WTI fell 1.27% to $91.11 on the announcement and ended the week at −1.92%, from $92.89 to $91.11, having been as low as $89.30 on Tuesday and as high as $92.87 on Thursday. Brent finished at $102.25. For the book the implication is unchanged and now firmer: crude is neither pushing the long end up nor pulling it down, so the rate problem cannot be blamed on energy and cannot be solved by it.
