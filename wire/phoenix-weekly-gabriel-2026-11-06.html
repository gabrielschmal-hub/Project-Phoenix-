A week of very large days in both directions. The S&P fell 2.68% at 2.40σ and WTI 13.23% at 2.27σ, both two-sigma weeks. The Nasdaq fell 3.37% at 1.82σ, gold rose 5.02% at 1.66σ, the Russell fell 2.05% at 1.45σ and the Dow 0.94% at 1.03σ.

The indices, precisely. The S&P fell 203.21 points to 7,376.86, a fourth straight weekly loss; the Nasdaq 857.58 to 24,579.42, now in a correction; the Russell 57.73 to 2,754.11; and the Dow 484.58 to 51,285.73. WTI fell from $104.12 to $90.34 and spot gold rose from $3,875.84 to $4,070.41.

Five sessions. Monday: WTI −7.36% after Iran said talks were authorised; Palantir beat its guide after the close and slipped. Tuesday: election day, chips +2.63%, AMD beat after the close, and the US struck Iran's Qeshm Island. Wednesday: Democrats won both chambers and the Dow rose 3.03%, its best day since April 2025, while WTI fell 8.29%. Thursday: the S&P fell 4.36% and the Nasdaq 5.52%, the worst day since April 2025, as chips fell 11-12% on data-centre fears and the VIX hit 28.18. Friday: payrolls of 165,000 against 59,000 expected, and a narrow chip rebound.

Bonds moved on the Fed, not on stocks. The two-year fell about 12bp on Thursday on soft labour costs and gave 5bp back on Friday's jobs report, closing the week at 4.51% on Treasury's curve; the ten-year ended at 4.86%. December hike odds swung from about 72% on Monday to two-thirds on Thursday and back near 78% on prediction markets after the payrolls.

What actually matteredfive sessions, filtered

The election changed the AI trade, and the jobs data kept the Fed hawkish

1. A Democratic Congress turned into a data-centre question. The market first read gridlock and bought, with the Dow up 3.03% on Wednesday. A day later it focused on what Democratic control and local votes could mean for data-centre buildout; UBS put about 30% of planned 2028 additions at risk, and chip and data-centre suppliers fell 10-20% in a session. Friday's bounce recovered only a third of that, and Trump's backing for the build-out did not settle it.

2. Oil gave back its war premium. WTI fell 13.23% as Iran authorised talks, Vance blamed the war for Republican losses and Qatar circulated proposals. No deal has been signed and Hormuz has not reopened; the price now assumes progress that has not yet happened.

3. The Fed's December decision is back on a hike. Soft labour costs briefly pulled the odds down on Thursday, but 165,000 jobs and 4.1% unemployment on Friday pushed them back near 78%. Cheaper oil helps on inflation, but the labour market is not cooling.

The war · the weekWTI $104.12 → $90.34

Talks authorised, strikes on Qeshm, and an election that raised the pressure

The week opened with Iran's first concrete opening in weeks. On Monday Iran said its supreme leader had authorised peace talks and offered to reopen Hormuz under strict conditions, and WTI fell 7.36%. On Tuesday the US struck Iran's Qeshm Island after Iran fired at US aircraft, while Vance said he and Trump felt good about the prospects for a settlement.

The vote added pressure to settle. Vance blamed the war for Republican losses, Qatar said it expected feedback within 36 hours, and Iran called the US reply to its Hormuz plan unconvincing. WTI fell 8.29% on Wednesday. By Friday Tehran was reported to be reviewing mediator proposals while seeking sanctions relief and an end to the US blockade.

Crude finished at $90.34, down 13.23% on the week. Thursday brought a small rebound as Eurasia Group cut its odds of a deal and tanker attacks continued. For this book the channel is the same: crude feeds inflation, and inflation feeds the Fed.
