The business in depth

A digital-only bank for young, high-earning professionals — lending, deposits and investing in one app — plus a technology platform it sells to other fintechs.

What it sells

Lending — personal loans (the largest line), student-loan refinancing and home loans; increasingly originated and sold to private-credit buyers through its Loan Platform Business. Financial Services — SoFi Money (checking/savings, funded by its bank charter), SoFi Invest, credit cards, crypto. Technology Platform — Galileo and Technisys, providing payment and banking infrastructure to other fintechs and banks.

Who buys it — customers

Over 11 million members, mostly younger with above-average incomes. Galileo's customers are fintech companies and banks that issue cards and run accounts on its rails.

Who it depends on — suppliers

Its funding: customer deposits (the bank charter lets it fund loans more cheaply) and private-credit investors such as Blue Owl and Fortress who buy its loans. Credit bureaus and data providers for underwriting.

Who it fights — competitors

Lending: LendingClub, Upstart, and big banks such as Chase and American Express. Deposits and investing: Ally, Chime, Robinhood, Schwab. Platform: Marqeta, Fiserv, FIS.

Why this matters for the tradeSoFi is a rate- and credit-sensitive lender: higher rates squeeze loan demand and gain-on-sale margins, and a weaker consumer raises losses. A hiking Fed is the worst macro for it. Earnings 27 October, the day before the Fed.

What the chart shows

From April to September SoFi traded between roughly $14.92 and $19.74. The tops have fallen since August — $19.74, $19.50, $18.35, $17.96, $16.42 — while the floor held. This week it broke below it to a new 52-week low of $13.92, on twice-normal volume, RSI 13.

The pattern call — and what to do with it

Falling tops over a flat floor is a descending triangle, and this week is the down breakout, confirmed if Friday closes below $14.92. Bulkowski: down breakouts rank 15/36, average decline 15%, and those near the yearly low do well. Measure rule: 50% of the ~$4.80 height gives ~$12.50. An extreme RSI is not a reason to buy into a confirmed breakdown before earnings.

Bulkowski · Descending triangleUp breakouts 53% (63% if price rose into it): rank 33/39, failure 22%, avg rise 38%. Down: rank 15/36, failure 23%, avg decline 15%, target met 50%. Breakout usually 61–65% of the way to the apex; price turns at the apex 60% of the time. A triangle resting on support is an easy win; overhead resistance kills up breakouts. Busted breakouts lead to powerful moves the other way.
