The business

Software that sits between an organisation's messy data and the decisions it needs to make — sold to governments first, and now to enterprises far faster.

Palantir sells an ontology: a structured model of how an organisation actually works, layered over data that was never designed to fit together. AIP puts language models on top of that structure. The distinction that matters commercially is that the output is auditable and the customer's data never becomes anyone else's training set — which is the entire pitch to a defence agency or a regulated enterprise.

US Government — the foundation

$809m, +90% in Q2. Includes the US Army's NGC2 common data baseline — the Army's highest-priority modernisation programme — and a DIA contract now clear of bid protest.

Customer: defence and intelligence agencies. Long procurement cycles, extreme switching costs once embedded.

US Commercial — the acceleration

$764m, +149% year over year. Record $2.132bn of total contract value signed in the quarter — nearly $800m above the previous best.

This is the number that moved the stock. Bookings ran far ahead of revenue, which is why guidance rose more than the quarter beat by.

International — the gap

US revenue is now over 81% of the total. International commercial has consistently lagged and remains the weakest part of the story.

A concentration risk dressed as a growth opportunity — and the reason "sovereign AI" is the strategic framing management now leads with.

Guidance rose by roughly $500m — far more than the quarter beat by $135m. That gap is the bookings, and it is why the reaction was 30% rather than 8%. Ninth consecutive EPS beat.

The valuation problem, stated plainly

Consensus modelling has the company at roughly $10.8bn revenue and $3.6bn earnings by 2028 — which requires 40.7% annual revenue growth and more than tripling earnings from $1.1bn.

One published fair value on those forecasts lands at $185.70 — roughly 3% above Friday's close.

In other words: after a 46% three-week advance, the price already embeds two more years of 40%+ compounding. That does not make it wrong. It removes the margin for a single disappointing quarter.

Before the print, this was a broken stockOn 3 August PLTR traded at $123 — down 40% from its peak and down 29% year to date — having fallen 14% after a Q1 report that also beat. Options priced a 10–12% earnings move; the stock delivered 30%. A name that punished eight consecutive beats and then gapped 30% on the ninth is not a name with a stable relationship between results and price. That history belongs in any position-sizing decision here.
What has been signed alongside the numbersMercury Systems — announced the same day as earnings — embeds Palantir's ontology into defence-supplier material planning and a digital twin of factory operations. Zeta Global is rearchitecting its Data Cloud on Foundry, which Zeta says could drive over $100m of its own annual revenue. Surf Air Mobility expanded its SurfOS partnership, with Wheels Up as launch customer. The pattern is consistent: Palantir is becoming the substrate other companies build their products on, which is a stickier position than selling seats.

Why this section exists. Implied volatility is a forecast; realised volatility is a measurement. When they diverge, the options market and the tape disagree about how much room a position needs — and a stop placed on the wrong one is either harvested by noise or wider than the account can carry. Across the eleven names reviewed this weekend, nine had implied below realised, several by more than fifteen points. Default to the measured range unless the two agree.

Reading the table: Stop dist is how far the stop sits from entry as a percentage of the share price — it is not the percentage of the account at risk. Account risk is 1.00% in every row, fixed at $20 on a $2,000 book. Share count is derived from it: shares = $20 ÷ (entry − stop). A wider stop buys fewer shares, not more risk. The only figure that varies with stop distance is position value, which is why the concentration cap and the 1R budget are separate constraints on separate axes.

The near target is too near

The sizing conflict

A 1-ATR stop gives 2.45 shares — $440, 22% of the account, in a stock that gapped 30% in a session three weeks ago.

Widening the stop to 2 ATR brings the position to a comfortable 11% — and drops the ratio to 1.24:1, below the commission floor. The two constraints pull against each other.

Modest improvement, still 22% of the book. This name is genuinely awkward for a $2,000 account.

The answer

The best growth reading anywhere in this series — 93% revenue growth, 149% US commercial, a 155% Rule of 40, $1.22bn of quarterly free cash flow and a record $2.13bn of bookings. 79/100, held back almost entirely by valuation. Nothing about the business argues against it.

What argues against it is that the market has already repriced it. Up 46% in three weeks, 28% above the 10-week, 27% above a 30-week that is still falling — the most extended reading of any name reviewed. A stop that clears the gamma flip is 4.5% down; the near_high trigger is 6.1% up. 1.36:1, in a stock that gapped 30% in a session three weeks ago and 14% the wrong way after the previous beat.

And there is a trap worth naming. near_high is only 6.1% away, so if the advance continues it flips before the 30-week turns. That would show three of five gates passing on a stock even further extended — the stack looking better while the risk got worse. The condition to wait for is consolidation that lets the 30-week catch up, not more upside that makes a gate go green.
