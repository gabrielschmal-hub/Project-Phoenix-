The business in depth

A young fabless chipmaker selling the connectivity chips that move data between GPUs, CPUs and memory inside AI servers.

What it sells

Aries PCIe and CXL retimers (keep signals clean over longer distances in servers — its original product); Taurus smart Ethernet cable modules; Scorpio fabric switches for AI racks (the newest and largest opportunity); Leo CXL memory controllers; and COSMOS software that monitors the links. Its chips sit inside AI servers built around Nvidia GPUs and the hyperscalers' own accelerators.

Who buys it — customers

Hyperscalers and AI platform makers, often reached through the server ODMs (Quanta, Wiwynn, Foxconn) that build the racks; programmes tied to Nvidia-based systems, Amazon Trainium, Google and Microsoft. A few customers make up most of revenue.

Who it depends on — suppliers

Fabless: TSMC manufactures its chips; packaging and test via partners such as ASE.

Who it fights — competitors

Broadcom (PCIe switches, Ethernet), Marvell, Credo (active electrical cables), Microchip, Montage, and Nvidia itself, whose NVLink competes with open alternatives.

Why this matters for the tradeAstera's growth depends on design wins in each new AI server generation — especially whether Scorpio switches get adopted — which makes revenue lumpy and the stock extremely volatile. Earnings 3 November.
