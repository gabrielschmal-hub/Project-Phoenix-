The business in depth

The leading maker of the etch and deposition machines that carve and layer every advanced chip — a direct read on how much the world's fabs are spending.

What it sells

Wafer fabrication equipment: etch systems (where Lam is the market leader — critical for 3D NAND's stacked layers and gate-all-around transistors) and deposition systems (laying down thin films). Plus the Customer Support Business Group — spares, upgrades and services on a large installed base, a steadier revenue stream.

Who buys it — customers

The world's chipmakers: TSMC, Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron, Intel, Kioxia, and Chinese fabs. Memory makers are disproportionately important because etch intensity rises with NAND layer counts. China has been a very large share of sales and is constrained by US export controls.

Who it depends on — suppliers

Precision subsystems and components: MKS Instruments, Advanced Energy, Ichor, Ultra Clean, plus machined parts, ceramics and vacuum components from specialist suppliers.

Who it fights — competitors

Applied Materials (the broadest equipment maker) and Tokyo Electron in etch and deposition; KLA in inspection and ASML in lithography are adjacent; Chinese challengers Naura and AMEC are growing inside China.

Why this matters for the tradeLam is a second-derivative AI play: it sells to the memory and logic makers whose own capex depends on AI demand. It reacts to chipmakers' capex guidance — TSMC's and the memory makers' — and to any change in China export rules. It reports Wednesday.

What the chart shows

Lam rose from $211 in April to about $400 in June, with a one-week spike to $438.50. Since then: a low of $250.50 in August, a higher low of $265 in September, and highs that have stayed under ~$357 — $345 in September and $352 in October. This week it has dropped 13% to $275, onto the rising lower line.

The pattern call — and what to do with it

Converging lines — lower highs and higher lows — make a symmetrical triangle. Bulkowski is blunt: it is one of the worst performers in his tests (rank 36/39 up, 34/36 down), notorious for busting in both directions, often more than once. Price is on the lower line now; a weekly close below ~$272 would be a down breakout. With earnings Wednesday, the print will likely decide the direction — and the pattern's track record says not to trust the first move.
