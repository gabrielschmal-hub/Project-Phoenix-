The business in depth

BlackRock's spot bitcoin ETF — the largest of its kind — giving brokerage-account investors direct exposure to the price of bitcoin.

What it sells

A share in a trust that holds bitcoin, for a 0.25% annual fee. Shares are created and redeemed daily by large broker-dealers, keeping the price close to the value of the bitcoin held. There is no business underneath: the ETF's performance is bitcoin's, less the fee.

Who buys it — customers

Retail investors, financial advisers and their clients, hedge funds, pension and endowment allocators, and corporate treasuries — anyone who wants bitcoin exposure inside a regulated brokerage account. Flows into the ETF are a visible gauge of institutional demand.

Who it depends on — suppliers

Coinbase Prime is the bitcoin custodian; BNY handles cash custody and fund administration; authorised participants such as JPMorgan and Jane Street create and redeem shares; market makers keep spreads tight.

Who it fights — competitors

Other spot bitcoin ETFs — Fidelity FBTC, Grayscale GBTC, ARK 21Shares, Bitwise; buying bitcoin directly on exchanges such as Coinbase; and equity proxies such as Strategy (MicroStrategy) and the bitcoin miners.

Why this matters for the tradeThe August dollar report found bitcoin trading as ~1.5-beta risk, not digital gold — a −0.88 correlation with gold at one point. That makes rates and risk appetite the drivers: a hawkish Fed on 28 October would hit it like a high-growth stock.
