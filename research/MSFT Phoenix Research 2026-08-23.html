The business

Three franchises that each individually would be a large-cap company, bound by an enterprise sales motion nobody can replicate.

Microsoft sells software on subscription to almost every large organisation on earth, then sells them the cloud it runs on, then sells them the AI layer on top. The moat is not any one product — it is that the buyer is already under contract.

Intelligent Cloud — Azure

The growth engine and the reason the stock is a proxy for AI capex. Reported robust growth alongside AWS at +37% and Google Cloud at +82%.

Customer: the enterprise IT budget. Supplier: NVIDIA, and the same memory market squeezing everyone.

Productivity — the annuity

Microsoft 365, Teams, LinkedIn, Dynamics. Recurring, price-escalating, and the vehicle through which Copilot reaches a billion seats without a new sales cycle.

This is the cash flow that funds the capex without debt — the structural difference from Alphabet's position.

More Personal Computing

Windows, Xbox, Surface, search advertising. Slower, cyclical, and increasingly a distribution channel for the other two rather than a growth story of its own.

Memory cost inflation lands here hardest, on hardware margins.

Why this section exists. Implied volatility is a forecast; realised volatility is a measurement. When they diverge, the options market and the tape disagree about how much room a position needs — and a stop placed on the wrong one is either harvested by noise or wider than the account can carry. Across the eleven names reviewed this weekend, nine had implied below realised, several by more than fifteen points. Default to the measured range unless the two agree.

Reading the table: Stop dist is how far the stop sits from entry as a percentage of the share price — it is not the percentage of the account at risk. Account risk is 1.00% in every row, fixed at $20 on a $2,000 book. Share count is derived from it: shares = $20 ÷ (entry − stop). A wider stop buys fewer shares, not more risk. The only figure that varies with stop distance is position value, which is why the concentration cap and the 1R budget are separate constraints on separate axes.

Why buying here fails

The $500 open-interest wall is 3.5% overhead and the near_high trigger is 5.7% away — so the first two objectives sit almost on top of each other, while the nearest defensible stop is 5.4% below.

1.06:1. That is what buying 13.8% above the 10-week costs.

Why waiting works

A pullback into the $440 area — the 10-week, rising fast to meet price — gives a 1.15 ATR stop and 4.44:1 to the gate trigger, 7.19:1 to the old high.

Same trade, four times the ratio, purely from not chasing a recovery at its widest extension.

breakout-traps-execution-filters · R1Never buy the first tick above a level — and never buy 14% above a rising short average. Extension is the mirror image of a stop harvest: the crowd that is early gets the good fill, and the crowd that chases provides their exit liquidity. On this chart the good fill is $440, not $483.
