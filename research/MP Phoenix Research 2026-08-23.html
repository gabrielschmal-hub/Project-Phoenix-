The business

The only vertically integrated rare earth producer in the United States, being built with public money into a strategic monopoly.

MP mines rare earth ore at Mountain Pass in California, separates it into oxides on site, converts those into metal and alloy in Texas, and is building the capacity to finish magnets. No other company outside China does all of that. The investment question is not whether the assets are valuable — it is whether the execution timeline justifies the price.

Materials — the producing business

Mountain Pass mining and separation. Q2 revenue $95.6m, +155%, adjusted EBITDA $32.5m. NdPr production 840 MT (+41%), sales 1,006 MT (+127%).

Customer: Sumitomo distribution, plus a named US technology and industrial offtake. Constraint: the ore body is light rare earth rich and heavy rare earth poor.

Magnetics — the thesis

Independence in Texas, plus the 10X campus at Northlake. Q2 revenue $16.5m, −17% as start-up costs displaced precursor sales.

GM qualification advancing, first commercial magnet deliveries expected in Q4. This segment is where the valuation lives and it is currently shrinking.

The government position

The Department of War is the largest shareholder via preferred equity and warrants, with a Samarium project loan and an offtake agreement alongside.

Plus a $110/kg NdPr price floor through 2035, settled quarterly in cash. That is an unusual asset: a decade of downside protection on the core commodity.

What has actually been signed

The anchor contracts

  • Department of War, July 2025 — equity, profit sharing, price floor, offtake. J.P. Morgan arranged $1bn of committed financing. The government became largest shareholder and largest customer at once.
  • Apple, $500m — recycled magnets from 2027, sourcing 100% of Apple's recycled rare earth magnets domestically
  • General Motors — qualification advancing, first commercial deliveries guided to Q4
  • Sumitomo — distribution agreement

Signed since the print

  • Gadolinium offtake with an unnamed leading US aerospace and defence customer — long-term, and the first heavy rare earth product agreement
  • Project Swarm, launched to aggregate and standardise magnet demand for drones and autonomous systems. Multiple US and allied customers subscribed
  • White House announced over $2bn of new critical minerals, magnets and battery investment on 11 August

Project Swarm is the interesting one: it is demand aggregation, not a single contract — MP setting the specification for an industry that does not yet have one.

The bottleneck, and where it still bindsRare earths are not geologically rare; separation and magnet-making are the scarce steps, and China holds the overwhelming share of both. MP has crossed the separation bottleneck for light rare earths — Mountain Pass is producing and selling. It has not yet crossed the magnet bottleneck, which is exactly what Magnetics revenue falling 17% while start-up costs hit $14.4m tells you. First commercial magnets in Q4 is the single event that would change the business.
The geological limit nobody can engineer aroundThe Mountain Pass ore body is rich in light rare earths and poor in heavies — dysprosium and terbium, which high-temperature magnets require. MP is commissioning a heavy separation circuit, but it separates what the rock contains. Full US independence in high-performance magnets is not achievable from this deposit alone, and that caveat survives every contract signed.

Reading the table: Stop dist is how far the stop sits from entry as a percentage of the share price — it is not the percentage of the account at risk. Account risk is 1.00% in every row, fixed at $20 on a $2,000 book. Share count is derived from it: shares = $20 ÷ (entry − stop). A wider stop buys fewer shares, not more risk. The only figure that varies with stop distance is position value, which is why the concentration cap and the 1R budget are separate constraints on separate axes.

It fits the account

Unlike TSLA or AAPL, the low share price means a proper stop still leaves a sane position: $209 at a 1.5 ATR stop, 10% of the book.

Sizing is not the objection here.

The ratios clear

2.51:1 to the $70 wall on a 1-ATR stop, 4.26:1 to the 26-week high. Both comfortably above the 1.70R commission floor.

Reward-to-risk is not the objection either.

So what is?

Entering after a +9.65% session, 15.9% above a falling 10-week, in Stage 1 at best, on a name with 69% volatility and a thin option chain.

The stage. Every time, the stage.

Why a good ratio on a bad stage is the most expensive trade there isThis is the setup that breaks discipline: the arithmetic works, the position fits, the story is compelling, and the stock just moved 9.65% in a day. Every one of those facts is an argument to override the gate stack, and the gate stack exists precisely for this moment. A 2.51:1 setup taken in Stage 1 with a 39% hit rate still loses money, because the ratio assumes the target is reached — and in a base, most breakouts fail back into the range. The ratio is real; the probability behind it is not.
If it were tradeable, the structure would be an entry after a pullback holds above $58.90 (the flip), stop below the $55 put shelf, first target $70. That is worth writing down for later — it is not worth taking now.

The answer

A genuinely strategic asset — the only integrated US rare earth producer, with the government as largest shareholder, a $110/kg price floor to 2035, and contracts with Apple, GM and an unnamed defence customer. Revenue grew 89% and adjusted EBITDA turned positive. 66/100.

And it is not a trade for this book, for a reason worth being precise about. The reward-to-risk works — 2.51:1 to the $70 wall on a 1-ATR stop. The position fits — $209 at a sane stop, 10% of the account. Neither of the usual objections applies.

What fails is the stage. Both passing gates are price extended above averages that are still falling, after a +9.65% session, 40% below the high, in a name running 69% volatility on a thin option chain. A working ratio on a Stage 1 chart is the setup most likely to make you override the system, and that is exactly what the stack is for. The trigger to watch is the 30-week turning up — not the next headline.
