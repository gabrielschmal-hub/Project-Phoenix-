The business in depth

America's only large memory-chip maker — and, through high-bandwidth memory, one of the three companies every AI accelerator depends on.

What it sells

DRAM (~75% of revenue): standard server and PC memory, mobile LPDDR, and HBM — high-bandwidth memory stacked next to AI GPUs, the highest-margin and most supply-constrained product in the industry. NAND: data-centre SSDs (fast-growing with AI storage), client SSDs and mobile storage. Sold through compute/networking, mobile, storage and embedded/auto units.

Who buys it — customers

Nvidia (HBM for its GPUs) and AMD; hyperscalers buying server DRAM and SSDs (Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta); PC makers (Dell, HP, Lenovo); smartphone makers (Samsung, Apple, Chinese OEMs); automakers and industrial firms.

Who it depends on — suppliers

Equipment: ASML (lithography), Applied Materials, Lam Research, Tokyo Electron, KLA. Wafers: Shin-Etsu, SUMCO. Its own fabs in the US (Idaho, New York under construction), Japan, Taiwan and Singapore, supported by CHIPS Act grants. Memory is capital-intensive: Micron is a giant customer of the equipment makers.

Who it fights — competitors

DRAM: Samsung and SK Hynix (the HBM leader), with China's CXMT rising at the low end. NAND: Samsung, SK Hynix/Solidigm, Kioxia, Sandisk, and China's YMTC. Three players control ~95% of DRAM, which is why pricing swings so violently.

Why this matters for the tradeMemory is the most cyclical corner of semiconductors: prices rise when supply is short, and every producer then adds capacity. The 2026 run was driven by an HBM and server-DRAM shortage; the question for the chart is whether that shortage is peaking. Sandisk falling 17% the same week says the market is asking it of the whole sector.

The pattern call — and what to do with it

If confirmed, his measure rule (51% of head-to-neckline height) targets the high $600s. A fast rise into the pattern, as here, gives the largest declines.
