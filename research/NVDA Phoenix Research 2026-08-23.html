The business

Not a chip company any more. An AI-factory supplier selling systems, networking and software as one stack.

NVIDIA sells the compute substrate that every frontier AI model is trained and served on. What makes it defensible is not the silicon alone — it is that CUDA, the networking, and the systems software are the same purchase, and switching means rewriting the stack.

Data Center — effectively the whole company

Over $30bn per quarter and the large majority of revenue. From Q1 FY27 it splits into Hyperscale and ACIE — AI clouds, industrial and enterprise.

Customer: the hyperscalers, plus a fast-growing tier of AI-native clouds. Supplier: TSMC, and an HBM memory market in shortage.

The reporting change matters

Two platforms — Data Center and Edge Computing — replacing the old segments. Management framed it as reflecting future growth drivers.

Splitting out ACIE is a tell: it lets investors see demand that is not the four largest buyers, which is the concentration question the bear case rests on.

Automotive and Omniverse

DRIVE AV in full production; DRIVE AGX Thor shipping. Omniverse libraries for physical AI, and an expanded Siemens partnership for factory digitalisation.

Small against Data Center, but it is the optionality that justifies part of the multiple.

Reward-to-risk

There is no honest R:R across a binary event. Here is why, in numbers.

Every ratio below is fiction until Thursday. An earnings gap is not bounded by ATR and does not respect a stop — the fill is wherever the open is. Presenting a reward-to-risk table for a position held through the print would be dressing up a coin flip as a setup.

Reading the table: Stop dist is how far the stop sits from entry as a percentage of the share price — it is not the percentage of the account at risk. Account risk is 1.00% in every row, fixed at $20 on a $2,000 book. Share count is derived from it: shares = $20 ÷ (entry − stop). A wider stop buys fewer shares, not more risk. The only figure that varies with stop distance is position value, which is why the concentration cap and the 1R budget are separate constraints on separate axes.

Why a stop does not protect you

A stop is an instruction to sell at market once a level trades. On an earnings gap the first print is the new price — there is no path through the stop level to fill against.

Implied volatility of 39.2% over three sessions implies a one-standard-deviation move of roughly ±7%, or $15. That is more than four times the stop distance in the first row.

What the wait costs

If the print is good and price gaps to $225, the post-event entry is worse than today's by about 5%. That is the cost of the discipline, and it is real.

It is also the correct price to pay. The library's own arithmetic: at a 39% hit rate a single −2R gap costs more than three winners return. Never take a binary event with a thin edge.

geometry-of-risk-sizing · gap exposureThe stop defines intended risk, not realised risk. Across a scheduled binary the two diverge by an unbounded amount, and the only control that still functions is not having the position. On a $2,000 book where 1R is $20, a 7% adverse gap on a full-size NVDA position is roughly 2.0R — double the intended loss, from one decision.
