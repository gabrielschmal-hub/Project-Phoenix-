The business

A car manufacturer valued as a technology company, with the gap between those two things now the entire investment question.

Tesla sells vehicles, energy storage, and a promise. The vehicle business is competitive, cyclical and capital-intensive. The promise — autonomy, robotaxi, humanoid robotics — is what supports a multiple no other manufacturer commands. The stock trades on which of those the market is weighting this month.

Automotive — the actual revenue

The overwhelming majority of sales. Margin has been the pressure point for two years as price cuts defended volume against Chinese competition.

Customer: retail vehicle buyers, increasingly price-sensitive. Supplier: batteries and the same memory and semiconductor cost inflation hitting everyone.

Energy — the quiet compounder

Megapack and storage deployment, growing faster than vehicles and at better incremental margin. Directly levered to the same data-centre power demand driving the AI infrastructure cycle.

The most underrated part of the business, and the only segment whose demand driver is currently accelerating.

Autonomy and robotics — the multiple

FSD, robotaxi and Optimus. No meaningful revenue line, and the reason the stock is valued at many times any automaker comparison.

Uber's approach is the useful contrast: it partners with over 20 AV providers and owns the demand layer. Tesla is building the whole stack alone.

The ratio is not the reason to decline this one. The position size is.

Reading the table: Stop dist is how far the stop sits from entry as a percentage of the share price — it is not the percentage of the account at risk. Account risk is 1.00% in every row, fixed at $20 on a $2,000 book. Share count is derived from it: shares = $20 ÷ (entry − stop). A wider stop buys fewer shares, not more risk. The only figure that varies with stop distance is position value, which is why the concentration cap and the 1R budget are separate constraints on separate axes.

The account mismatch

A 1.00-ATR stop is $14.60. Against a $20 risk budget that is 1.4 shares — a $497 position, 25% of the account, from a single stock with 47.6% realised volatility.

Widen the stop to something appropriate for the volatility and the position becomes too small to be worth the commission. That is the trap: the two constraints pull in opposite directions.

The commission arithmetic

At a $241 position, €1 round trip is 0.45% of position value. On a $497 position it is 0.22%.

Both clear 0.054R against the risk budget — but the smaller version needs a much larger percentage move to matter at all. High-priced, high-volatility names are the worst fit for this book.

tail-risk / concentration · and the sizing ruleThis is the clearest case in the series of a name that does not fit the account rather than does not fit the setup. Even if every gate passed, a stock with a $14.60 daily range at $363 forces a choice between a stop inside the noise and a position over the concentration cap. Note it as a structural exclusion, not a timing decision — the answer will be the same at $400 and at $300.
