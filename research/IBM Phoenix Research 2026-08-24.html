01

The business

A software and consulting company with a mainframe attached — and in the June quarter the mainframe did the damage.

IBM sells enterprise software on subscription, consulting hours to deploy it, and the Z mainframe that a large share of the world's transaction processing still runs on. The July crash was not a software problem. It was a mainframe cycle turning down at the same moment enterprise budgets moved to AI hardware — and a management team that did not see it coming.

Software — the part that worked

$7.76bn, +5.1%. Segment profit +9% to $2.50bn, margin up 110bps to 32.2%. Annual recurring revenue reached $24.6bn, +8%. Hybrid cloud +11%.

This segment grew, expanded margin and grew its recurring base. Nothing in the software numbers explains a 25% decline.

Infrastructure — the damage

Mainframe revenue −42%. Z and Transaction Processing software both fell short. A product-cycle trough compounded by clients deferring.

Mainframe revenue is lumpy by design — it spikes on a new Z generation and collapses between them. The问题 is that the trough arrived unannounced.

Consulting — the swing factor

Large deals slipped out of the quarter. Krishna cited "delayed deals" and industry-wide cybersecurity distractions pulling client attention and budget.

Whether that revenue was delayed, reduced or lost is the open question — and the one the market decided not to wait for an answer on.

Quarterly dividend held at $1.69. Consensus FY26 EPS around $12.33. The business is profitable, cash-generative and guiding to growth — which is precisely why the credibility question, not the arithmetic, is what remains unresolved.

The context that makes the reaction legibleQ1 2026 revenue grew ~9% to $15.92bn and beat. One quarter later growth was 1.1% and the company pre-announced a miss. A deceleration from 9% to 1% in a single quarter, disclosed off-schedule, is not a data point — it is a statement that management's visibility into its own pipeline was wrong. That is what a 25% decline prices, and no single quarter repairs it.
The legal overhang — and its dateA securities-fraud investigation opened in the wake of the crash and remains active; a further legal notice was published on 21 August, the most recent trading day in this report. The question under investigation is whether IBM misled the market about the strength of its deal pipeline before the 14 July warning. This is not a quantifiable risk with a resolution date — it is an open-ended headline exposure sitting on top of an already-broken chart, and it is the single clearest reason this name should not be a discretionary override candidate.
One forward-looking item worth knowingOn 14 August IBM opened a new enterprise distribution channel for a major AI model provider, positioning itself as the intermediary for large corporate clients deploying frontier AI inside regulated, secure, hybrid-cloud environments. That is the correct strategic response to the exact problem that caused the miss — if budgets are moving to AI, be the channel. It is far too early to appear in revenue.

A 50-point spread — and why it is not a free lunchImplied volatility is 61% below realised. On the surface that says options are extraordinarily cheap. The honest reading is more careful: realised vol of 82.8% is dominated by a single 25% session inside the 30-day window, and that event does not repeat — the same arithmetic will read very differently once 14 July rolls out. What the spread does tell you with certainty is that an options-derived stop would be less than half the width this stock actually requires. The IV-implied daily move is $4.75; the measured range is $11.54. Size from the second number, and note that IV sitting at the 0th percentile of the last 13 weeks means the market has already stopped pricing follow-on risk from an investigation that is still open.
Why this section exists. Implied volatility is a forecast; realised volatility is a measurement. When they diverge, the options market and the tape disagree about how much room a position needs — and a stop placed on the wrong one is either harvested by noise or wider than the account can carry. Across the twelve names reviewed, ten had implied below realised. IBM's 50.8-point gap is the widest by a factor of two. Default to the measured range unless the two agree.

The answer

Fails every gate, four weeks after the largest single-day decline in a listing history stretching back to 1916. Price sits below the 40-week, 30-week and 10-week, all three falling, 29% from the two-year high. 63/100 — second lowest of the twelve names reviewed.

The business underneath is not broken: software grew 5.1% with ARR up 8% at a 32.2% margin, free cash flow is guided higher, the dividend held. A 3.7% revenue miss produced a 25% decline because the market repriced credibility, not one quarter — and a company that went from 9% growth to 1% in three months, disclosed off-schedule, cannot repair that with arithmetic.

Two things make this the clearest decline of the series. The securities-fraud investigation into pre-warning pipeline disclosure remains open — a fresh notice was published on Friday — so the mechanism that produced a 25% gap is still live and undated. And implied volatility has already collapsed to 32% against 82.8% realised, sitting at the 0th percentile of the last 13 weeks: the options market has stopped pricing follow-on risk from an investigation that has not concluded. That is not cheap insurance. It is a market that has moved on before the story has.
