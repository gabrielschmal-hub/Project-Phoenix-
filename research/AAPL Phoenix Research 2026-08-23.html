The business

A hardware installed base that has become a rent-collection machine, now spending heavily to avoid being disintermediated by AI.

Apple sells devices once and monetises them continuously. The installed base reached an all-time high across every product category and every geography this quarter — and that base, not the quarterly unit count, is the actual asset.

iPhone — the engine

$54.3bn, +22% in the June quarter — half of all revenue. June-quarter records in every geography and a record for upgraders.

Customer: ~1.5bn active devices. Supplier: TSMC for A19 silicon, and a memory market whose costs rose enough to force price increases.

Services — the margin

$30.7bn, +12% — a June record, and a miss against $31.22bn expected. Roughly 28% of revenue at far higher margin than hardware.

App Store, iCloud, Music, TV+, payments, and the Google search default. This line is what the 50.1% gross margin rests on.

Mac, iPad, Wearables

Mac $10.35bn, +29% — a large beat, driven by the MacBook Neo, the low-cost laptop built on an iPhone chip. iPad $6.19bn missed; Wearables $7.88bn.

The Neo is the interesting one: Apple silicon economics applied to a price point it could not previously serve.

Guidance is the problem

September quarter revenue guided to +9-11%, decelerating from +16%. A 2.5-point FX headwind and — the unusual part — supply constraints on iPhone, Mac and iPad simultaneously.

Cook cited less flexibility in the supply chain than normal and constrained availability of advanced nodes. The stock fell 6% after hours on this, not on the quarter.

What has actually shipped and changed

Products and platform

  • All-new Siri AI unveiled at WWDC26 — the centrepiece, and the thing the multiple is being asked to pay for
  • R&D up 32%, a hybrid of third-party cloud and Apple's own data centres
  • MacBook Neo — iPhone-chip laptop at a new price point, and the driver of a 29% Mac quarter
  • New child-safety features shipped alongside the OS update
  • Installed base at an all-time high in every category and geography

Corporate

  • CEO transition — this was Tim Cook's final earnings call
  • $600bn US reshoring commitment, with supplier implications still working through
  • Dividend raised to $0.27 — a 14th consecutive annual increase
  • Price increases taken, forced by exponential memory cost growth
  • EPS has beaten in each of the last eight quarters; this was the largest beat of the run, and the most inflated
The single question that mattersIs Siri AI good enough? The 32% R&D increase, the multiple, and the "AI inflection" framing all rest on it. Early beta commentary is mixed at best. This is a case where the product review cycle is the financial catalyst — and unlike a supply constraint or an FX headwind, it cannot be modelled, only observed.
Two structural changes worth holding in mindA CEO transition and a $600bn reshoring commitment are both multi-year uncertainties that do not show up in a quarterly model. Neither is a reason to avoid the stock; both are reasons the multiple may stay compressed while the market waits for evidence.

Reading the table: Stop dist is how far the stop sits from entry as a percentage of the share price — it is not the percentage of the account at risk. Account risk is 1.00% in every row, fixed at $20 on a $2,000 book. Share count is derived from it: shares = $20 ÷ (entry − stop). A wider stop buys fewer shares, not more risk. The only figure that varies with stop distance is position value, which is why the concentration cap and the 1R budget are separate constraints on separate axes.

Apple is up 14.1% this year into a sector up 22.2% — an 8-point deficit. Read that with care: at a top-three index weight, Apple is a large component of the thing it is being compared against, so the gap measures the rest of technology outrunning it rather than Apple lagging an independent benchmark. At 89.8% of its 52-week high against a sector at 98.4%, Apple is the laggard in a leading group rather than a leader in a lagging one. The tape is not what is holding this back; the September guidance is.
For a top-weight index name, the sector is the wrong referenceApple is a top-three weight in the S&P 500, so the causation largely runs the other way. It does not follow technology; it is a substantial part of what technology and the index are. For names of this size the governing context is the index itself and the index gamma regime — SPX dealer positioning moves AAPL through index-level hedging flow, ETF creation and redemption, and the passive bid, none of which pass through XLK first.

The rule of thumb: the higher a name's correlation to SPX, the more the index and its gamma regime govern it; the lower the correlation, the more sector and industry matter. For the top 20-30 index weights the sector comparison is close to decorative. It becomes genuinely informative somewhere past the top 50, and it is the primary reference for a mid-cap like SOFI, which has 33 points of relative deficit to XLF that is entirely its own.

This run did not retrieve SPX price data or SPX gamma. Both belong here for a name this size and their absence is the real gap in this section — not the missing industry taxonomy.
