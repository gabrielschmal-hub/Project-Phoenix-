The business

Solid-oxide fuel cells sold as on-site power — and the AI buildout has made them the answer to its hardest bottleneck.

Bloom makes fuel cells that generate electricity on site, without waiting for a grid connection. That was a niche industrial product for two decades. Then AI data centres started hitting multi-year interconnection queues, and speed-to-power became worth more than cost-per-kilowatt. Bloom did not change; the value of what it sells did.

Product — the engine

$935m, +215% year over year in Q2. Product gross margin 37.2%, up 291bps. Manufactured in the United States.

Customer: all major US hyperscalers plus a dozen-plus neoclouds, AI labs and colocation operators. Supplier risk: scandium, named on the call as a capacity constraint.

Service — the quiet compounder

Service margin 22%, up 977 basis points year over year. Sridhar singled it out: 80% of 2025 orders came from repeat customers.

Recurring revenue attached to an installed base is what separates an equipment cycle from a durable business, and it is the least-discussed line here.

The financing structure

Brookfield expanded its framework from $5bn to $25bn — a fivefold increase in nine months. Oaktree, MUFG, Morgan Stanley and IDF also named.

Bloom does not carry the project risk. Financiers are contractually obliged to take delivery, which is how a capital-intensive product scales without a capital-intensive balance sheet.

Why customers are switching, in the company's own wordsSridhar said customers who had already contracted for combustion turbines or reciprocating engines are cancelling those orders and moving to Bloom, naming Nebius as one such convert. He also claimed "very high 90s" market share in fuel-cell-based data centre power. That is a competitive claim about a category, not about the whole power market — turbines and engines remain the larger competitor, and the switching evidence is the thing to track rather than the share statistic.

In a market where speed-to-power is the product, a 40% installation reduction is a competitive claim rather than a press release.

Reading the spreadRealised volatility of 130.2% is the highest recorded across this library by a wide margin — the next closest is INTC at 81.3%. It means the stock's ordinary daily move is 8.2%. Implied volatility at 89.5% is far below that, and sits at the 4th percentile of its own 52-week range — the options market is pricing the calmest conditions of the year into a stock that has moved 618% and then given back 38%. A stop sized on implied would be roughly 31% too tight. Use the measured range: $22.59 a day.
Why this section exists. Implied volatility is a forecast; realised volatility is a measurement. When they diverge, the options market and the tape disagree about how much room a position needs. Across sixteen instruments reviewed, thirteen had implied below realised. BE's 40.7-point gap is second only to IBM's post-crash reading. Default to the measured range unless the two agree.

The fractional-share problem

A 1-ATR stop buys 0.89 shares. At 1.5 ATR it is 0.59. Fractional shares make this executable, but consider what it means: a $192 position where one ordinary day moves it $20 — a full 1R — in either direction.

The stop is one normal session wide. That is not a stop; it is a coin flip with commission attached.

The 4.71:1 is the same mirage as MP

The headline ratio to the near_high trigger looks excellent. It requires a 49% advance in a stock trading at 266× earnings, 38% below a June high, with an average analyst target below spot.

A ratio computed against a target the stage says is unreachable is arithmetic with the probability removed.

The structural exclusion — and why it is not a timing decisionThis is the same finding recorded for TSLA, and it is more extreme here. At 10.4% daily ATR, no stop exists that is both wide enough to survive normal noise and tight enough to leave a position worth holding. Widen it to 2 ATR and the position is $96 — on which €1 of commission is 1.0% of position value, nearly twenty times the 0.054R cost floor as a share of capital deployed. Tighten it and the stop sits inside a single day's range. Record BE as a structural exclusion at this account size, not a name to time. The answer does not change at $150 or $300.
