The business in depth

A cloud data platform: companies load their data into Snowflake, query and share it, and increasingly run AI on it — paying for what they use.

What it sells

The AI Data Cloud: data warehousing (its original product), data lakes (Iceberg tables), data engineering, data sharing and the Marketplace, and AI services — Cortex and Snowflake Intelligence, which let customers run language models over their own data. Priced by consumption: revenue is credits used, not seats sold.

Who buys it — customers

Over 11,000 organisations, with revenue concentrated in large enterprises spending $1m+ a year — banks, retailers, media, healthcare, technology firms. Consumption means revenue tracks how much customers actually query, so it responds quickly to IT-budget tightening or optimisation.

Who it depends on — suppliers

Snowflake runs on AWS, Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud — its largest cost and, awkwardly, its competitors. Model providers Anthropic, OpenAI, Meta and Mistral supply the models in Cortex.

Who it fights — competitors

Databricks is the main rival, strongest in data engineering and AI. The clouds' own warehouses: AWS Redshift, Google BigQuery, Microsoft Fabric. Also Oracle, Teradata, and open-source engines (ClickHouse, DuckDB) that undercut on price.

Why this matters for the tradeConsumption pricing makes Snowflake a fast read on enterprise data and AI workloads — the reason it doubled from April. It also means guidance can change quickly. Earnings around 2 December.

What the chart shows

Snowflake bottomed at $118 in April, gapped from $172 to $256 on May earnings, and climbed to the low $330s by late August. It then traded sideways between roughly $304 and $349 for nine weeks (with a one-week spike to $384). On 9 October it closed at $368.89, above the range. This week it is back to $319, inside it.

The pattern call — and what to do with it

The nine-week range is a rectangle entered from below. October's close above the top was the breakout — and this week's return inside makes it a bust. Bulkowski: busted breakouts lead to powerful moves in the new direction, and his rectangle data shows down breakouts are much weaker performers than up (rank 32/36 vs 4/39) — so a bust toward the $304 floor is the expectation, not a collapse. A weekly close back above $349 would repair it.
