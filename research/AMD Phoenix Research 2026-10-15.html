The business in depth

The only company that competes with Intel in x86 processors and with Nvidia in AI accelerators at the same time — and is winning share in both.

What it sells

Data Center is the largest segment: EPYC server CPUs, where it has taken a large share from Intel, and Instinct AI GPUs (MI350, MI400 coming) plus rack-scale systems from the ZT Systems acquisition. Client: Ryzen PC processors. Gaming: Radeon graphics and the custom chips inside Sony's PlayStation and Microsoft's Xbox. Embedded: Xilinx FPGAs and adaptive chips for industrial, auto and telecom.

Who buys it — customers

Hyperscalers and AI labs for Instinct and EPYC — Microsoft, Meta, Oracle, and OpenAI, which agreed in late 2025 to deploy multiple gigawatts of AMD GPUs. PC makers Dell, HP, Lenovo. Sony and Microsoft for console chips. Industrial and automotive firms for embedded.

Who it depends on — suppliers

TSMC makes every leading AMD chip — the single critical dependency. High-bandwidth memory from Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron; advanced packaging capacity at TSMC; server manufacturing via partners such as Sanmina, which bought ZT's factories.

Who it fights — competitors

Nvidia in AI accelerators (by far the leader); Intel in server and PC CPUs; Arm-based custom CPUs from Amazon (Graviton), Google (Axion), Microsoft (Cobalt); custom AI ASICs designed by Broadcom and Marvell; Qualcomm in Windows-on-Arm laptops.

Why this matters for the tradeAMD's multiple now rests on its AI GPU ramp — whether the MI400 generation lands on time and the OpenAI deployment converts to revenue on schedule. Server CPU share gains are the steadier base. Hyperscaler capex on 28–29 October and AMD's own print on 3 November are the two tests.

What the chart shows

AMD went from $192 in March to $516 by June — +155% in about ten weeks. It then moved sideways for four months between roughly $424–440 and $585. In October it broke out, closing three weeks above $585 and reaching $658.52. This week it has fallen back to $560 — below the old range top.

The pattern call — and what to do with it

The spring move is a high and tight flag pole: price more than doubled in under three months. The four-month range is the (wide) flag, and October was the breakout. Bulkowski: only ~14% of these break down, but he warns that throwbacks hurt and that loose flags underperform tight ones — this one is loose. A weekly close back below $585 would turn the breakout into a throwback-failure; holding above it keeps the pattern intact. Either way the stop structure is far below.
