The business in depth

The second-largest designer of custom AI chips after Broadcom, and the leader in the optical-signal chips that connect AI servers to one another.

What it sells

Data Center (~75% of revenue): custom AI accelerators ("XPUs") co-designed for hyperscalers; electro-optics — DSPs and drivers inside the 800G and 1.6T optical modules that link GPUs across a cluster; Ethernet switches; and storage controllers. Smaller lines in enterprise networking, carrier infrastructure, consumer and automotive Ethernet.

Who buys it — customers

The hyperscalers: Amazon (Trainium-related custom silicon), Microsoft (Maia), Google and Meta. Optical module makers such as Coherent, Innolight and Eoptolink buy its DSPs. A handful of customers account for a large share of revenue.

Who it depends on — suppliers

Fabless: TSMC makes its leading chips; advanced packaging and HBM are sourced through TSMC and the memory makers for custom accelerators.

Who it fights — competitors

Broadcom in custom AI chips and switching; Nvidia in networking and as the alternative to custom silicon; Credo in connectivity; Asian ASIC design houses Alchip and GUC; MediaTek.

Why this matters for the tradeRevenue is lumpy because custom-chip programmes are won and lost one at a time — a single hyperscaler's design choice can move the next year. The optical business grows with cluster size, which makes it a steadier proxy for AI capex. Earnings are not until about 1 December.
