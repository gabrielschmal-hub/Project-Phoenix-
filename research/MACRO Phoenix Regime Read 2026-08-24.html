Yields at a 20-month high.
The dollar at a three-month low.
Those two do not normally coexist.

The single most important fact in this market is a broken correlation. Rising US yields normally pull capital in and lift the dollar. Right now the 10-year is at 4.74%, a twenty-month high, while the dollar index sits near 98.8, a three-month low, beneath a falling 200-day average. When yields rise and the currency falls together, the yield rise is not a growth premium or a policy premium. It is a risk premium. Investors are demanding more to hold dollar assets rather than competing to buy them.

01

How to read this report

A regime read is not a forecast. It is a statement about what is priced, and where the pricing and the mechanism disagree.

Macro edge does not come from being right about the economy. It comes from the misalignment between what a causal chain implies and what an asset is actually priced for. A macro view that agrees with current pricing is not tradeable, however correct it turns out to be. This report therefore does three things: establishes what each asset is priced for, traces the transmission between them, and then names the specific divergences worth acting on — or states plainly that there are none.

The discipline this document is held toEvery regime read must terminate in a named divergence or an explicit nothing-to-do, and must carry written invalidation conditions. Without both, the read cannot be judged right or wrong afterwards — only liked or disliked. Section 09 contains the divergences; section 10 contains what would falsify them.

The new chair changes the mechanism, not just the personnelKevin Warsh has abandoned forward guidance and defended doing so, noting that nominal and real yields have risen across the curve and attributing that to markets focusing on data rather than on the Fed. His phrase — "market participants are learning to play the ball and not the referee" — is a deliberate policy choice with a direct market consequence: without guidance, every data release carries more price impact than it used to. That is why volatility around CPI and PCE prints should be expected to be higher in this regime than in the last one, and it is a structural reason to be flat into 11 September rather than positioned.
Read both ends of the curve, not one rateThe short end prices policy expectation; the long end prices inflation and growth. The 2-year at 4.24% sits above the 3.50–3.75% funds rate — the short end is already carrying tightening. The 10-year at 4.74% and the 30-year at 5.25% are doing something different: they are pricing term premium, issuance and fiscal risk. A single-rate read would conflate the two and miss that the long end is rising for reasons the Fed does not control.
03

Bonds — where the stress actually is

The long end is selling off, the Treasury is intervening, and the relief is temporary.

The curve today

2-year4.24%
10-year4.74% · 20-month high
30-year5.25%
10s–2s spread~+50bp, steepening

A positively sloped, steepening curve with the long end leading is a bear steepener — the least comfortable of the four curve shapes. It says the market wants more compensation for duration, not that it expects faster growth.

Why the long end is under pressure

  • AI capital spending is being debt-financed. Alphabet alone raised $22.93bn in early August with maturities to 2066; Meta, Oracle and others have issued into the same window
  • Federal deficit spending continues, though Treasury Secretary Bessent argues the deficit has peaked
  • Oil at $80 on Iran sanctions feeds directly into inflation expectations

The first of these is new and underappreciated. Hyperscaler capex is now large enough to move the Treasury curve — corporate issuance competing with government issuance for the same duration buyers.

The authorities are already intervening — and it is not holding

What has been done

  • Treasury to at least double buybacks of 10–30 year securities, to $4bn next quarter, with Bessent signalling it could exceed that
  • A request for the Fed to raise the limit on its FIMA facility
  • A joint intervention on the Japanese yen, executed by selling euros

What it achieved

The 10-year fell, then "more than erased the decline" and returned to test 20-month highs within days.

Three separate official actions to compress long yields, and the yield is higher after them than before.

This is the most important paragraph in the report. When authorities act to suppress a price and the price goes the other way, it is evidence about the size of the flow they are fighting. It does not mean intervention cannot work; it means the current scale is not sufficient. Watch for escalation — a larger buyback programme, or an explicit yield objective — as the signal that the fiscal channel has become the dominant policy problem.

The dollar — the broken correlation

Falling while yields rise. That combination has a specific meaning.

Where it is

DXY~98.8–99.6
Position3-month low
200-day MA~102.4 · declining
RSI~49 · neutral
Structurerising channel support

Below a falling long average, testing channel support. On the same weekly framework used for single names, the dollar would fail trend200 on level and slope.

Why it matters that it is falling

The normal chain: US yields rise → dollar assets pay more → foreign capital flows in → dollar strengthens. DXY and the 10-year are normally positively correlated.

What is happening instead: the 10-year is at a twenty-month high and the dollar is at a three-month low.

The only coherent reading is that the yield rise is compensation for risk rather than reward for growth. Reporting attributes the dollar's weakness explicitly to concerns over rising US government debt.

The distinction that governs everything downstreamA "good" yield rise reflects stronger growth or a more hawkish central bank. It lifts the currency, and equities can absorb it because earnings are rising alongside. A "bad" yield rise reflects term premium — investors demanding more to fund a deficit or absorb issuance. It weakens the currency, and equities cannot absorb it because the discount rate rises without the earnings. The dollar is the tell that separates the two, and right now it is pointing at the second.
05

Oil — the transmission channel

Not an asset class here. The mechanism that turns geopolitics into a rate decision.

Where it is and why

WTI~$80
DriverIran sanctions
StructuralHormuz supply chains

The US is preparing sweeping new economic sanctions against Iran. J.P. Morgan named a "slower-than-expected normalisation of supply chains around the Strait of Hormuz" as one of two reasons it moved to a September hike as base case.

The chain, stated explicitly

Iran sanctions → crude to $80 → headline inflation firms → passes into core through input costs → the three FOMC dissenters gain a data argument → September hike odds rise → short end sells off → the whole curve reprices.

This is why oil belongs in a rates report. It is currently the single most direct link between a geopolitical event and the Fed's September decision.

Two caveats that stop this becoming a one-way argumentFirst, seasonality. A crude curve read as a stress signal without checking it against a seasonal norm is unreliable — in seasonal markets the curve often reports the calendar rather than the fundamentals. Second, the demand side. A supply-driven price rise into a softening labour market is not the same as a demand-driven one; it is more likely to be treated by the committee as a level shift than a trend. The Fed's own framing in the minutes was about "understanding underlying inflation amid shocks" and the degree to which shocks are broadening. That word — broadening — is what to watch in the PCE detail.

The single-name evidence that supports the budget-rotation thesisThis is not a theory drawn from the index. It is what the individual reports found. IBM fell 25% in a day — its worst session since 1916 — on a 3.7% revenue miss its CEO attributed explicitly to clients redirecting late-June budgets toward servers, storage and memory. Three in five software stocks fell with it. Meanwhile memory contract prices are up 78–83% quarter-on-quarter, Apple guided to simultaneous supply constraints across iPhone, Mac and iPad, and NVIDIA carries a memory-cost margin risk into Wednesday's print. One rotation, visible in five separate companies' disclosures.
Why a 2% drawdown with 12.65% implied vol is the position, not the reassuranceEquities are not mispriced because they are high. They are exposed because they are calm. Implied volatility in the 21st percentile means options are cheap, hedges are cheap, and very little of the fiscal or policy risk described above is embedded in the index price. That is the definition of an asymmetry: the downside case requires a repricing that has not happened, while the upside case requires a continuation that is largely already in the price.

Scenarios — what happens if

Each with its transmission chain, not just its direction.

Scenario A — The Fed hikes in September

Currently ~31% on prediction markets, and J.P. Morgan's base case. Requires August CPI on 11 September to firm, most plausibly through energy passing into core.

AssetDirectionMechanism
Short rates2-year to 4.50%+Direct repricing; the front end already carries some of this at 4.24%
Long ratesambiguousThe key nuance. A credible hike can lower the 10-year by restoring inflation-fighting credibility — a bull flattener. If instead the long end rises with the short end, that says the market does not believe one hike is enough
DollarhigherRestores the broken correlation — and this is the cleanest confirmation that the credibility problem was the driver
Equitieslower initiallyHigher discount rate with no earnings offset. Long-duration and unprofitable names worst — PLTR, INTC, MP, TSLA

The counter-intuitive part worth holding onto: a hike could be bullish for bonds and bullish for the dollar at the same time as being bearish for equities. Watch the 30-year: if it falls on a hike, the market is saying credibility was restored. If it rises, the market is saying one hike does not fix the fiscal problem — and that is the worse outcome.

Scenario B — The dollar keeps falling

The current trend. Below a falling 200-day, at a three-month low, on debt concerns. Continuation is the path of least resistance absent a hike.

ConsequenceDirectionMechanism
CommoditieshigherPriced in dollars. A weaker dollar is inflationary through import and energy costs — and feeds straight back into the hike case
Long yieldshigherThe reflexive loop: weaker dollar → imported inflation → higher yields → more fiscal concern → weaker dollar
US large-cap earningshigher, reportedRoughly 40% of S&P revenue is overseas. A weaker dollar mechanically flatters translated results
Gold and hard assetshigherThe classic debasement expression, and the one most consistent with the fiscal reading
Foreign demand for TreasuriesweakerThe dangerous leg. An overseas holder loses on the currency and the price simultaneously

This scenario is self-reinforcing, which is what makes it the one to respect. A falling dollar raises inflation, which raises yields, which raises fiscal concern, which lowers the dollar. Breaking that loop requires either a hike or a credible fiscal announcement — and Bessent has signalled a forthcoming fiscal plan.

Scenario C — The Fed holds and inflation keeps cooling

The ~69% case, and what equities are currently priced for. July CPI at 3.4% headline and 2.5% core cooling, payrolls declining — the disinflation continues and the committee stays put.

AssetDirectionMechanism
Short rateslowerThe 2-year gives back the tightening premium above the funds rate
Long ratesmay not followThe trap in this scenario. Issuance and term premium are not a Fed problem. The 10-year can stay near 4.74% even as the 2-year falls — a steepener that keeps pressure on equity multiples
DollarlowerNo rate support, fiscal concern intact — feeds Scenario B
EquitieshigherRelief, led by the same long-duration names that would suffer most in Scenario A

The important warning here: a hold is only bullish for equities if the long end cooperates. If the 2-year falls and the 10-year does not, the market has told you the problem was never the Fed.

Scenario D — The tail: a disorderly long end

Low probability, high consequence. The 30-year is already at 5.25%. Three official interventions have failed to hold yields down.

The trigger would be a failed or badly-tailed long-bond auction, a further large AI-related debt issue landing into a weak bid, or an inflation print that forces the market to price several hikes at once. The consequence is a simultaneous decline in bonds, equities and the dollar — the configuration in which diversification stops working, because the common factor is the credibility of the issuer rather than the economic cycle.

The diagnostic that distinguishes this from an ordinary risk-off: in a normal equity selloff, long bonds rise. If equities fall and long bonds fall with them, the panic thesis is refuted and this is the scenario in progress. That single check — do bonds rally? — is the most valuable real-time indicator in this report. Caveat: in an inflation shock both fall together, so this is a strong prior rather than a law.

09

The named divergences

Where pricing and mechanism disagree. This section is the point of the document.

Divergence 1 — the dollar is telling a different story from the bond market's own narrativeBonds are selling off, which is conventionally read as a growth or hawkish-policy story. If that were true the dollar would be rising. It is at a three-month low. The two markets cannot both be right, and the currency is usually the better witness because it is the cleanest expression of cross-border willingness to fund. The tradeable implication: positioning that assumes "higher yields = stronger dollar = pressure on gold and commodities" is positioned against the actual mechanism. Watch: if a September hike lifts the dollar, the credibility reading was right.
Divergence 2 — equity volatility does not reflect rate volatilitySPY implied volatility sits at the 21st percentile and matches realised. Meanwhile the 10-year is at a twenty-month high, the 30-year is above 5%, September hike odds have swung from 79% to 31% inside a month, and the Fed has abandoned forward guidance — which mechanically raises the market impact of every data release. Rate uncertainty is elevated and equity-implied uncertainty is not. That gap is either an opportunity to hedge cheaply or evidence that equities genuinely do not care about rates until a level is breached. Historically, that assumption survives until it does not, and the transition is abrupt.
Divergence 3 — the software/semiconductor decoupling is a budget signal, not a sector rotationThe IGV–SOXX correlation at 0.17, the lowest since 2002, is being treated as sector rotation. The single-name evidence says it is something more structural: enterprise hardware spending is consuming capital that previously funded software licences. IBM lost a quarter of its value on that mechanism in a single session. If it is a rotation, it mean-reverts. If it is a budget reallocation, it persists and software multiples are still too high. Wednesday's NVIDIA print is a direct test: strong hardware demand confirms the reallocation and is structurally negative for enterprise software even as it is positive for semis.
Divergence 4 — official intervention is failing and the market is not pricing escalationThree separate actions — doubled buybacks, an FIMA request, a coordinated yen intervention — and the 10-year is higher than before them. Markets are treating this as noise. It is information about the size of the flow the authorities are fighting. The asymmetry: if the next step is a materially larger programme or an explicit yield objective, that is a regime change in how US duration is priced, and very little in current pricing anticipates it.
And the explicit nothing-to-do. For a $2,000 systematic long-only breakout book, none of these four divergences is directly tradeable. They are not equity breakouts, they require instruments outside the mandate, and two of them resolve on a single data print three weeks away. The correct use of this document is not to trade it. It is to explain why thirteen consecutive single-name reviews produced zero candidates — and to time the next review around 11 September rather than before it.

Invalidation

What would prove this read wrong. Written before the event, not after.

This read is wrong if

  • The dollar rallies while yields keep rising. That restores the normal correlation and refutes the risk-premium reading entirely — the single cleanest falsification
  • The 10-year falls below 4.40% without a Fed cut, showing the issuance concern was overstated
  • August CPI on 11 September comes in soft and the long end rallies with the short end — a bull flattener would say fiscal risk was never the driver
  • The Treasury's buyback programme visibly caps the 30-year below 5% for a sustained period
  • IGV–SOXX correlation normalises above 0.5, making the decoupling a rotation rather than a reallocation

This read is confirmed if

  • Equities and long bonds fall together on a risk-off day — the diagnostic from Scenario D
  • A September hike lifts the dollar sharply, confirming credibility was the binding constraint
  • The 30-year moves above 5.50% despite further intervention
  • Gold makes new highs alongside a rising 10-year — the debasement expression
  • Another large enterprise-software company reports a hardware-budget-driven miss
  • The Treasury escalates to a materially larger buyback or an explicit yield objective
The three dates that resolve most of thisWed 26 August — July PCE, the Fed's preferred gauge, and NVIDIA earnings the same evening. One print tests the inflation path, the other tests the budget-rotation thesis, on the same day. Fri 11 September — August CPI, which decides September. Tue–Wed 15–16 September — FOMC, with a Summary of Economic Projections that will reveal whether the dot plot has moved toward the dissenters. Everything between now and 26 August is positioning, not information.

What this means for the book

The specific consequences for a $2,000 systematic long-only breakout account.

1 · The gate stack is doing its job

Thirteen single names reviewed, zero full passes. That is not bad luck or an over-strict filter.

It is a market where the index is 2% off its high while individual names are 10–40% below theirs. Narrow leadership is exactly what a rising-discount-rate regime produces, and near_high is the gate that detects it.

2 · Be flat into 11 September

Without forward guidance, every data release carries more price impact. CPI on 11 September and the FOMC on 15–16 are two binary events inside four sessions.

On a book with a thin edge, a correlated gap across several positions is the fastest route to several R. The calendar is the risk control here.

3 · Rank what to watch after

If the hold arrives and the long end cooperates, the names that move first are the ones closest to their triggers.

AMZN $264.22 · NVDA $217.62 · AAPL $317 — the three with three gates passing and short distances to the fourth.

The one macro reading that changes a Phoenix ruleA rising-discount-rate regime with narrow leadership systematically penalises the long-duration, unprofitable, story-driven end of the market — PLTR, INTC, MP, TSLA in this series. It rewards cash generation now — AMZN, AAPL, MSFT. The gate stack already encodes this indirectly through stage2 and near_high, which is why the two lists came out the way they did. No rule change is warranted; this is the engine working as designed, and the macro read explains why rather than instructing a change.
The picture in five sentences

The Fed is debating a hike, not a cut, with three dissenters already voting for one and a new chair who has removed forward guidance. Long yields are at twenty-month highs for reasons the Fed does not control — issuance, deficits and AI-related corporate debt — and three official interventions have failed to hold them down.

The dollar is falling while yields rise, which identifies this as a risk premium rather than a growth premium — the single most important observation in this report. Oil at $80 on Iran sanctions is the channel converting geopolitics into a rate decision.

And equities are two percent off their high with implied volatility in the 21st percentile, priced for a hold and no multiple compression, with no margin in either assumption. Nothing here is tradeable by this book — and that is the finding. Be flat into 11 September.
