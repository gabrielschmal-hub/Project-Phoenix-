The business in depth

The retail brokerage app that brought commission-free trading to a generation of investors, now expanding into crypto, banking, retirement and prediction markets.

What it sells

Transaction revenue — mostly payment for order flow on options, equities and crypto trades; the most volatile line. Net interest revenue on customer cash and margin loans — directly rate-sensitive. Robinhood Gold subscriptions; retirement accounts; a credit card; Bitstamp (crypto exchange); tokenised stocks in Europe; and prediction markets on events.

Who buys it — customers

Some 25–27 million funded retail customers, skewed young; active traders via Robinhood Legend; increasingly wealthier users via Gold and retirement products. Activity — and therefore revenue — rises and falls with market enthusiasm and crypto prices.

Who it depends on — suppliers

Market makers — Citadel Securities, Virtu, Jane Street and others — who pay for order flow and execute the trades; banks that hold swept customer cash; crypto liquidity providers; event-contract partners.

Who it fights — competitors

Charles Schwab, Fidelity, Interactive Brokers and Webull in brokerage; Coinbase in crypto; Kalshi and Polymarket in prediction markets; eToro and Revolut internationally.

Why this matters for the tradeRobinhood's revenue is a function of retail risk appetite and interest rates — the same two variables the 27 October print and the 28 October Fed decision will move on consecutive days.
