The business in depth

The dominant supplier of chips for premium Android phones, and a patent-licensing business that collects royalties on most of the world's smartphones.

What it sells

QCT (chips), ~85% of revenue: Snapdragon processors and modems for handsets (the majority), automotive (Snapdragon Digital Chassis — cockpit, connectivity, driver assistance; the fastest-growing line), IoT and industrial, PCs (Snapdragon X for Windows laptops), and new data-centre AI inference chips. QTL (licensing): royalties on 3G/4G/5G patents — small in revenue, very high in margin.

Who buys it — customers

Android phone makers — Samsung, Xiaomi, Oppo, Vivo, Honor — plus Apple for modems, a relationship winding down as Apple ships its own C-series modems. Automakers including GM, BMW and Mercedes; PC makers for Snapdragon X; licensees paying royalties across the industry.

Who it depends on — suppliers

Fabless: TSMC and Samsung Foundry make its chips. It licenses the CPU instruction set from Arm — the relationship now in litigation, with a court ruling on possible royalty relief still unscheduled.

Who it fights — competitors

MediaTek in Android (strong in mid-range and gaining at the top); Apple's in-house modems; Samsung Exynos; Google Tensor. In PCs, Intel and AMD. In automotive, Nvidia and Mobileye. Arm itself is moving toward making its own chips.

Why this matters for the tradeLosing Apple's modem business is a known, scheduled decline; the growth story is auto, PCs and AI at the edge. The Arm ruling is the wildcard: it can change Qualcomm's royalty cost on every chip it sells, and it has no date.

What the chart shows

Qualcomm rose from $127 in April to $259.92 in May — doubling in about six weeks — and then fell almost all the way back, to $142.89 in August. It has since recovered to $205.86 in early October and pulled back to $170.

The pattern call — and what to do with it

The spring is a textbook V-top: a straight-line rise and a nearly equal fall. Bulkowski's target for a V-top is the start of the rise (~$127); price got within 12% of it. Since then the structure is a range from roughly $143 to $206, and the October high is a lower high well below the May peak. The three weekly averages are bunched between $170 and $178 — price is sitting on the bottom of that cluster. There is no bullish pattern here to trade; the undated Arm ruling could gap it either way.
